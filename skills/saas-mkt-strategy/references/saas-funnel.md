# The SaaS funnel and benchmarks

## Stages

| Stage | Demo-led | Trial-led |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Visit | Website visitors | Website visitors |
| 2 Ask | Demo requests | Trial sign-ups |
| 3 Engage | Demos held | Activated trials (reached the first useful result) |
| 4 Pay | New paying customers | New paying customers |
| 5 Stay | Retained customers (churn) | Retained customers (churn) |

A hybrid uses both rows at stages 2–3. Objectives sit on the stage with the biggest drop or gap
in S1; for a product losing customers, stage 5 comes first ("you can't outrun churn", Walling).

## Benchmarks: use with care

Benchmarks are other companies' numbers. They help judge whether a target is plausible; they
are never the founder's baseline. Every one used goes under `## Benchmarks used` with its source,
and each is labelled "benchmark" wherever it appears.

| Measure | Benchmark | Source | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free-trial to paid, B2B | median about 8%, very spread out (many under 2.5%, many over 25%) | Kyle Poyar, Growth Unhinged, 2026 free-to-paid report | Depends heavily on card-up-front and price |
| Customer lifetime value to acquisition cost | above 3 | David Skok (forEntrepreneurs); HubSpot; SaaS Hero | Needs churn and acquisition cost the founder knows |
| Payback on acquisition cost, small-business SaaS | under 12 months (Bessemer); 5–7 months is healthy (Skok) | Bessemer; Skok | Sources disagree; calculated in different ways (Ray Rike, SaaS Barometer) |
| Monthly revenue churn | above 2% a month is a problem | Skok | Annual plans hide monthly churn; use renewal rate |

If a measure isn't in this table and the founder gave no figure, write `[TBD]`; don't find one.
