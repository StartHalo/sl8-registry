# Review method

A consistency review checks material as it is produced, against the codified brand, with
structured findings rather than a general impression. "Number of issues" alone misleads; what
matters is which issues change how people see the brand.

## For each item

| Check | Against |
|---|---|
| Promise and message | the umbrella and supporting messages; one message per piece |
| Voice | each "X, not Y" attribute and the tone for this moment |
| Look | palette (by eye and, for supplied hex values, exact), type families, imagery and icon rules |
| Distinctive assets | present and used consistently |
| Health claims | the health-claims check |
| Store fields (if a listing) | the character limits |

## Severity

- **High:** the wrong promise, a health or medical claim risk, or so off-brand it reads as
  another company.
- **Medium:** voice or look drift a regular user would notice.
- **Low:** polish: wording, spacing, a near-miss colour.

## The three tests (Marty Neumeier)

- **Focus:** can you say who we are, what we do and why it matters from this material?
- **Hand:** cover the name and logo: is it still recognisably ours?
- **Swap:** put a competitor's name on it: does it still work? If so, it's not distinctive yet.

## Writing a finding

"Email subject 'Exciting news!!' · high · breaks 'calm, not hype' · before → after: 'Sleep
stories are here'". Quote the material exactly; never paraphrase it into a quote.
