# PMA method and bounded supplements

Read for create, resume, or update. The primary method is Product Marketing Alliance / James
Shaw's Product Launch Framework, identified as M1. Retain M1.S1–M1.S8 labels in the strategy so
the customer can distinguish covered work from unresolved decisions. PMA provides the planning
structure, not a guarantee of outcomes or current platform rules. SD1–SD5 have separate authorship.

## M1.S1 — Frame the launch project

State the problem, objective, scope, assumptions, constraints, accountable owner, desired change,
and evidence boundary. Separate an owner-approved target from an objective hypothesis. If dates,
resources, success thresholds, or owner are absent, name the decision required instead of choosing
them. Preserve an already completed frame on resume. On update, modify it only when changed
evidence affects it. Cross-cutting authority comes from the job's scope, not PMA or SD5.

## M1.S2 — Select the target audience

Compare candidate segments supported by the supplied record. For each, show needs, evidence,
counterevidence, limits, and implications. Recommend a provisional segment or state why selection
is unresolved. Distinguish behavior from occupation or demographic assumptions. Missing denominators,
deduplication, access, or consent prevent population claims. Preserve minority safety/usability
signals and contradictions even when positive feedback is more frequent. Customer approval and
new evidence requirements belong in decisions and research, respectively.

## M1.S3 — Establish positioning

State category, actual alternatives, differentiation, benefits, intended audience, and launch
hypothesis with atomic claim labels. Separate supplied product capabilities from claimed customer
value. When category or differentiation remains unresolved, apply **SD4**, April Dunford's
Obviously Awesome positioning investigation: actual alternatives → distinctive attributes →
customer value → customers who care most → market category. Use only supplied material; mark
prelaunch conclusions as hypotheses. If the evidence cannot answer a stage, show its gap. Keep
the supplement conditional and obtain human positioning approval before claiming a decision.

## M1.S4 — Define and learn through product testing

Define beta goals, evidence needed, timeline inputs, feedback collection/review, and readiness risks.
Distinguish a reported defect, successful reproduction, confirmed fix, and verified retest; do not
collapse them. Propose the product/testing owner's next action and explicit evidence condition.
No testing access, consent, contact, data collection, or product change is implicitly authorized.
Apply SD3 only using supplied applicable current platform evidence. Missing evidence becomes a
research request; do not imply a test or platform check was performed.

## M1.S5 — Decide launch readiness

Apply the rule in [claims-and-readiness.md](claims-and-readiness.md), explicitly called
**SD5 — Factory readiness extension**. PMA supplies testing and planning inputs; Factory supplies
this inferred decision rule. Show recommendation, blocker class, evidence/freshness, owner,
completion condition, and the human decision request. Keep strategy, decisions, and state coherent.
An unknown prerequisite is unsatisfied. Favorable feedback cannot cancel a severe confirmed defect.

## M1.S6 — Build the launch plan

Create pre-launch, launch, and post-launch work with a dependency graph or table. For each work
item state deliverable, proposed/accountable owner, prerequisites, evidence of completion, schedule
or missing scheduling input, resources, budget status, and human authority needed. Distinguish
planned actions from completed work. Block dependent launch work behind unresolved readiness gates.
Apply **SD2 — Mobile Growth Stack**, attributed to Andy Carvell, to check acquisition,
activation/engagement, retention, monetization, and analytics. Do not reduce the plan to installs.
Apply SD3 to platform work and SD5 to readiness dependencies and authority.

## M1.S7 — Select and allocate launch tactics

Connect each candidate tactic to audience evidence, positioning hypothesis, objective, role, tools,
resources, prerequisites, measurement, and budget status. Explain uncertainty and evidence that
would change the choice. With weak acquisition evidence, apply **SD1 — Bullseye**, attributed to
Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares: propose small time-boxed channel tests before any scale decision.
For each test propose the hypothesis, eligibility/consent needs, owner, measurement, stop/learn
condition, and missing owner-approved time or money limit. Do not pick a proven winning channel or
fabricate an approved allocation. SD3 constrains platform-dependent tactics; SD5 preserves authority.

## M1.S8 — Define and track GTM execution

Make the handoff understandable: market, channel, value/message, team, ownership, and review cadence.
For each proposed metric state definition, unit, numerator/denominator where relevant, time window,
source, collection/access owner, freshness, and current availability. Use the actual platform's
supplied definitions; Apple and Google metrics are not interchangeable. Unavailable data is not zero.
Cover SD2's lifecycle dimensions and SD3's platform constraints. Under SD5 propose 24-hour,
7-day, and 30-day learning reviews relative to a human-authorized launch: who reviews which evidence,
what decision it informs, what condition would stop or revise activity. Do not invent measured
performance, approved thresholds, a launch date, or business outcomes.

## SD3 — Current platform constraints across M1.S4–M1.S8

SD3 is a named deviation using current Apple/Google documentation for the actual platform.
Use only supplied, dated, applicable platform evidence; a historical method source is not a current
policy check. Store/release requirements, testing tracks, privacy/attribution, measurement, and
rollout mechanics may need separate questions. When current evidence is absent, give the human a
bounded request for the exact primary source, jurisdiction/platform applicability, date/version,
and decision it would support. Do not browse or insert recalled policy as a verified fact.

## Completion and revisions

A step can be addressed with explicit unknowns and bounded next actions. This is planning completion,
not satisfied evidence or launch approval. Record which obligations were completed; do not claim
completion for omitted sections. On resume retain completed decisions; on update identify affected
steps and dependent SD1–SD5 work while preserving unrelated constraints. Do not substitute SOSTAC,
LOVED, or an anonymous blend for M1.
