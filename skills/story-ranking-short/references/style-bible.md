# Style bible: tone and what good looks like

SL8-authored, untested (the vendor's style file was never served, research
everygen/rankingvideos-1aa4ce#3). Glance at it while writing the script; it sets tone, not rules.

## The format
- A fast vertical countdown: each item is one short phone-footage moment with one clear physical
  payoff, cut hard into the next. The on-screen title names the ranking; the list on the left fills
  in one line per item, so the viewer watches #1 arrive.
- No narrator, no captions, no music bed, no intro card, recap, end card or call to action. The
  sound is what each clip makes: a pop at its start, the filmer's reaction, a bass hit on the impact.

## Tone clusters

| Cluster | What it feels like | Payoffs that work |
|---|---|---|
| comedic fails (default) | friendly slapstick; nobody is hurt | things fall, spill, splash, collapse, miss |
| satisfying wins | clean, precise, earned | a perfect catch, a fit, a stack that holds |
| cute moments | warm, surprising | an animal or small child does the unexpected harmless thing |
| extreme skills | impressive, rising difficulty | a trick lands, a shot goes in |

## What good looks like
- Every clip passes as a real phone video; the joke is the event, never the camera work.
- Each item is a different place, person and kind of payoff; the list reads well on its own.
- #1 is the biggest: the most visible, most surprising result, still harmless.
- Generic everyday people; no injuries, blood, danger to children or animals, or humiliation of a
  type of person.
