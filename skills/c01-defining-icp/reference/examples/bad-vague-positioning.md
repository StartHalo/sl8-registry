# Zipcar positioning statements: judged against this deliverable

Zipcar is an app-booked car-sharing service. The textbook excerpt teaches how a positioning statement can move from a competitive frame to a customer goal. As a marketing-strategy textbook illustration, that is a fair aim. **As an ICP and positioning one-pager for an app, it falls far below the bar.** It is shown here with the deliverable's fields filled only from what the source gives.

## Segment(s)
- **Who:** "urban-dwelling, educated techno-savvy consumers". In version 2 this becomes those "who worry about the environment that future generations will inherit".
- **Job hired for:** implied as not owning a car ("instead of owning a car").
- **Pains:** not stated beyond an implied cost and environmental worry.
- **Trigger:** not stated.
- **Where to reach:** not stated.
- **Evidence:** none given.
- **Number of segments:** one, defined by demographics and attitude.

## Positioning statement
Version 1: "To urban-dwelling, educated techno-savvy consumers [target], when you use Zipcar car-sharing service instead of owning a car [competitive frame], you save money while reducing your carbon footprint [points of difference]."

Version 2: "…you make a responsible choice [brand essence] and demonstrate your commitment to protecting the environment [target's goal]."

## Proof points
None. There are no figures and no reasons to believe for "save money" or "reducing your carbon footprint".

## Why it is bad
- The target is a demographic adjective string ("urban-dwelling, educated techno-savvy"). It has no count, no named city and no behaviour such as how often they need a car, so it cannot be sized or targeted.
- There is no trigger and no channel, so it cannot lead to an acquisition action (when to show the ad, where).
- "Save money" and "reducing your carbon footprint" are claims with no numbers, no comparison basis and no evidence type.
- The "unlike" is a whole category (car ownership) and there are no proof points. Version 2 moves even further into abstraction ("responsible choice").
- It has a single segment, so it gives no basis for choosing between segments or prioritising them.
