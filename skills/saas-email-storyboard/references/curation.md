# Curating the storyboard

Source: Jane Portman, "Atomic Emails: Our Proven Method for Writing SaaS Email Campaigns"
(Userlist, published 2024-05-23, updated 2026-05-19). The recipe, the purposes and "one email,
one idea" are hers. The customer re-read below is ours (her own seven questions were not
recovered in research), labelled as such.

## One email, one idea

Each email carries one idea and asks for one thing. If an idea needs two asks, it is two emails.

## The four purposes

| Purpose | What the email does | Example idea |
|---|---|---|
| Crucial problem | Removes an obstacle that stops the customer reaching the goal | "The one setting that stops double-booking" |
| Perceived value | Shows what the product is worth: a result, a comparison, a story | "How a 12-person team cut month-end by two days" |
| Inspiration | Makes the goal feel worth it and possible: the founder's why, a vision of after | "Why we built this after losing a weekend to spreadsheets" |
| Action | Asks plainly for the conversion event, with a reason to do it now | "15 minutes to set it up with me this week" |

## The recipe (Portman)

For a full sequence pick: **2–3** crucial-problem emails, **1–2** perceived-value, **1–2**
inspiration, **1–2** action. That is 5–9 emails. When the founder sets fewer, or the campaign type in
[cadence.md](cadence.md) runs shorter (a re-engagement sequence of 3–5, a launch of 2–3), keep at
least one crucial-problem and one action email, and add value before inspiration. A one-off broadcast is
one email whose purpose matches the goal.

## The customer re-read (ours)

Read each picked idea as the person in the segment, on a busy day, and keep it only if:

1. It is about their problem or result, not about the product's features.
2. They would understand why they got it from the subject alone.
3. It gives them something useful even if they don't click.
4. Its ask fits how ready they are at that point in the sequence.
5. Nothing in it would annoy them (guilt, fake urgency, "just checking in").

An idea that fails 1 or 5 is dropped. One that fails 2–4 is reworked or moved.

## Order

Order by the customer's journey (S1 steps to success): what they need to know first comes first.
Action emails usually come after at least one problem or value email; a demo-lead sequence may
open with a light ask if the lead already asked for a demo.
