# Delays by campaign type

Portman (Atomic Emails, Userlist, 2026) gives two ranges: onboarding emails go daily or every
2–3 days; nurture and customer-success emails go 1–4 weeks apart. The other rows are our
defaults, labelled ours; the founder can change any delay.

| Campaign type | Delay between emails | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Trial or onboarding (people using the product now) | 1–3 days | Portman |
| Lead nurture (interested, not using it yet) | 7–28 days | Portman |
| Re-engaging cold leads (asked once, went quiet) | 3–5 days for a short sequence of 3–5 emails | ours |
| Cold first contact (a prospect list the bot built) | 3–4 emails, 3–5 days apart; the first introduces the founder and the problem, the last says it's the last | ours |
| Launch or announcement | day 0, a follow-up 2–4 days later to non-clickers, a last call before any deadline | ours |
| Seasonal push (a buying window such as budget season or a new financial year) | 4–7 days, timed so the action email lands 2–3 weeks before the window | ours |
| One-off broadcast | a single send | ours |

Write delays as "day 0", "+3 days" (after the previous email). Name the send day only if the
founder gave a date; never invent a launch or deadline date. Avoid weekends for business
recipients unless the founder says otherwise.
