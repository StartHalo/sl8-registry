# Method: what a review checks, and where each rule comes from

Two sources. Cite the rule and its source in each finding.

## Atomic Emails (Jane Portman, Userlist, 2024-05-23, updated 2026-05-19)
- One email, one idea; one primary call to action (up to two secondary, or a P.S.).
- Each email has a purpose: a crucial problem, perceived value, inspiration, or action.
- Order follows the customer's journey; a person leaves the sequence when they reach the goal.
- The sender is a person; the voice is one person to one person.

## Litmus, "The Ultimate Email Checklist" (2022-09-20), by phase
- **Content:** the subject and preview text say why to open; no typos; facts are right.
- **Inbox view:** a recognisable from-name; the preview text is set, not left to the first line.
- **Design:** works on a phone and in dark mode; alt text on images; plain-text version; under 102 KB.
- **Links and tracking:** every link works and is tagged; merge tags have fallbacks.
- **Deliverability and compliance:** a working unsubscribe link and the sender's postal address in
  every email; opt-in for who receives it; sender authentication. Text alone cannot show headers or
  authentication: list them under "Verify outside the text".

## How the public reviews do it (Joe Daniels, Joe Versus Churn, 2020)
Quote the element, say what it does to the reader, give the fix, and say what to keep. See
`examples/ref-good-*.md`.
