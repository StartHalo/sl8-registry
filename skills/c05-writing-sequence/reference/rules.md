# Rules for every email

- **Facts:** every number, customer, quote, feature or offer comes from the request or a page of the
  product's site opened in this job, with its link in the Assumptions or the setup sheet. Nothing
  else is stated as fact. No invented results, testimonials or deadlines.
- **Postal address:** the founder's, or the placeholder. Never invented.
- **One idea, one call to action** per email; the button says what happens ("Connect your inbox").
- **The founder's voice:** "I" and "you", to one person.
- **No footer in the body:** build.mjs adds the unsubscribe link and the address as merge tags.
- **Contacts:** never collect, scrape, import or store anyone's email address or personal data.
- **Never send:** the founder loads and sends from their own tool.
- **Legal:** list what to verify in the send checklist; never say the sequence is compliant.

## Phrases that make an email read as generated

Live users of AI email tools complain that emails read "assembled rather than written", in one
tone everywhere (Retainful, 2026-03-16; Venture Harbour, 2026-09-23). Avoid these, and their
cousins.

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "I hope this email finds you well", "I hope you're doing well" | Filler before the point |
| "Just checking in", "Just following up", "Circling back", "Bumping this" | Asks for attention without giving anything |
| "Innovative", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "robust", "game-changer", "revolutionize", "unlock", "leverage", "synergy", "streamline your workflow" | Vague claims anyone could make |
| "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape" | Throat-clearing |
| "Don't miss out!", "Act now!", "Limited time" (without a real deadline from the founder) | Fake urgency; spam filters notice too |
| "Click here", "Learn more" as button text | Say what happens: "See the 2-minute demo" |
| "We're excited to announce", "We're thrilled" | About us, not them |
| "As per my last email" | Passive-aggressive |
| Three adjectives in a row; an em-dash in every paragraph; every paragraph the same length | Generated rhythm |
| ALL CAPS words, "!!", "FREE", "$$$" in subjects | Spam triggers |

## Customer check (each email, before it's done)

From Jane Portman's Atomic Emails (Userlist, 2026): write from the synopsis, shape a clear CTA
hierarchy (primary, secondary, P.S.), write the subject and preheader, then re-read as the
customer. The questions below are ours; Portman's own list was not recovered in research.

Read the email as one person in the segment, on their phone, between two meetings.

| Check | Fix if not |
|---|---|
| The first line is about them (their situation, problem or goal), not about us | Rewrite the opening |
| The subject alone tells them what's inside and why it's for them | Make it specific; drop cleverness |
| The preheader adds a reason to open, not a repeat of the subject | Rewrite it |
| There is one idea, and one main ask | Cut the second idea; save it as a leftover |
| They get something useful even without clicking | Add the one useful thing (a tip, a number, a step) |
| The ask fits where they are in the sequence (a soft ask early, a plain ask later) | Soften or sharpen the CTA |
| It sounds like the founder wrote it to one person | Remove marketing phrases; use "I" and "you" |
| Every fact has a source (a page opened, or the founder) | Remove the fact |
| Nothing pressures: no fake deadlines, no guilt, no "just checking in" | Remove it |
