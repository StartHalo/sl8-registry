# Research method (Wheeler's research phase, for one consumer app)

Wheeler's phase 1 runs: define the problem, market research, a marketing audit, a competitive
audit, a verbal audit, then a findings report. For one app with a small team, those become the
sections of `01-research.md`. Diagnose before deciding (Ritson): nothing here proposes the brand.

## Finding competitors

- Start from the person's list. Fill to 3–5 from the app's store category: search the store for
  the words in the app's own title and subtitle, and take the apps that appear for the same need.
- Prefer direct rivals (same need, same audience) over category giants. One giant is useful as a
  reference for the category's clichés.
- Record each competitor's listing URL. A competitor you couldn't open is listed as "not read",
  not described.

## Reading a store listing

| Field | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Name and subtitle (30 characters each on Apple) | the promise in fewest words |
| Icon | the one distinctive asset most people see |
| First screenshot and its caption | the main benefit shown |
| First three lines of the description | the promise in words; the voice |
| Rating and number of ratings | scale, not quality |
| Recent reviews | what users actually value and dislike |

Store pages are sometimes rendered by script and don't fetch. Try the plain listing URL once.
If it fails, say so and ask the person to paste the listing.

## Review themes (likes and dislikes)

- Read as many recent reviews as the page shows (or the person pasted); say how many.
- Group them into themes: what users praise, what they complain about, the words they use for
  the need. Users' own words are the best raw material for the brand's voice and promise.
- Quote short phrases exactly, with the source. Never paraphrase into a quote, never invent one,
  never count what you didn't read.

## Touchpoint audit

For each touchpoint you can see, ask: does it say the same promise, in the same voice, with the
same look, as the others? Inconsistency is a finding; so is a touchpoint the app doesn't have.

## Category clichés

List the words, colours and images most competitors share (for a wellness category often: soft
gradients, leaves, "your journey", "holistic", pastel purples). The brand will need at least one
thing none of them have.

## Questions for the person to ask users

When there is little audience insight, write at most 5 open questions the person could ask a few
users (for example "What were you using before this app, and why did you switch?"). The bot never
contacts users.
