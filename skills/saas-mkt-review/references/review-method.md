# Reviewing results

## Done versus achieved

For each S2 objective, keep apart:
- **What was done:** actions from S5 the founder says happened.
- **What buyers did:** visits, demo requests, trial sign-ups, replies.
- **What it achieved:** new paying customers, activation, churn, revenue, only as the founder says.

Compare each result with its baseline and target. Only the founder's figures count: a result that
wasn't supplied is "no figure supplied", never a guess.

## Channel tests (Bullseye)

For each channel tested, compare the result with the threshold S4 set before the test:
- **Continue** (cleared it): it becomes, or stays, the focus channel.
- **Change** (close, or the wrong kind of customer): what to change and re-test.
- **Stop** (well short): drop it this period; it goes back to B or C.

If none cleared its threshold, look at the message first (Weinberg and Mares: "look at the
messaging you've been using, or dig deeper to see at what point each channel failed") before
blaming the channel.

## Broken assumptions

Read the plan's Assumptions and the S1 key findings. For each one the results contradict, say
which figure contradicts it and what it means for the plan.

## Reflection (Asia Orangio)

One short paragraph: what the founder learned this period that the next plan should keep, and
what to stop doing.

## Steps to redo

Mark the earliest SOSTAC step the results make wrong, and why. Rules of thumb:
- the picture of customers or competitors was wrong → S1;
- an objective was badly off and its funnel stage was the wrong one → S2;
- the target, positioning or sales motion didn't land → S3;
- a channel failed its test, or one passed → S4;
- only timing, hours or budget → S5;
- only the measures → S6.

If the plan still holds, say "none: the plan stands", and why.
