# Reading the customer's voice

What stops a B2B buyer is usually a fear, an uncertainty or a doubt the page doesn't answer
(ResearchXL calls them FUDs): "will setup take weeks?", "can my team actually use it?", "what if
we're locked in?", "is our data safe?". Customers say these in their own words in reviews,
tickets, sales calls and interviews. Those words are the best material for the page.

## What to pull out of each source

| Pull out | Example of the shape (invented) |
|---|---|
| The trigger: what made them look | "our spreadsheet broke during the busiest week" |
| The fear before buying | "worried the staff wouldn't bother with another app" |
| What almost stopped them | "the price wasn't on the site, so I nearly didn't ask" |
| The outcome they value, in their words | "I stopped chasing people for updates" |
| Alternatives they compared | "we looked at doing it by email" |

## Confidence

Label every finding:

- **High:** the same point from 3 or more separate people or sources;
- **Medium:** 2 people, or 1 clear first-hand account;
- **Low:** one passing mention, or second-hand.

Fewer than 5 data points per buyer type is a small sample; say so. Name the file each point comes
from. Quote short phrases exactly; don't put words in customers' mouths.

## Bias checks

- Happy customers write reviews; people who left rarely do. Ask who is missing.
- Support tickets over-represent problems after buying, not before.
- Sales notes are the founder's own words about the customer, not the customer's.
