# Why most messaging frameworks fail

Four failure modes the article names: (1) collapse under sales pressure (no guidance for objections); (2) internal confusion (teams read it differently); (3) no defined enemy ("generic frameworks" give forgettable messaging that fits any rival); (4) "death in enablement" (shelfware in a shared drive).
Quote: "If your framework can't survive a pricing objection, it's not a framework, it's arts and crafts." It says most firms "treat frameworks as fill-in-the-blank templates."

Corroboration, same pitfalls list: Pragmatic Institute (Steve Piper), https://www.pragmaticinstitute.com/resources/articles/a-practitioners-guide-to-product-message-maps, undated: too many pillars (5+) or too few (2); "fluff and hype" instead of compelling attributes.

**Why it is bad (countable):** this is a critique, not a bad specimen; I found no public bad filled house. Countable bad signs it gives: a promise any rival could sign; 5+ or 2 pillars; claims with no proof; no per-audience line. Note: the author is B2B and sells services, so treat as a vendor's view.
