# S2 Objectives and S3 Strategy: the method

## S2 Objectives (PR Smith, with Ritson and Phiture)

- **At most 3 objectives.** Each is SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, timed.
- Put them on the app's own funnel: store view → install → activation → D30 retention → paid.
  Aim at the stage where the biggest drop is (from S1), not at "awareness" in general.
- Each objective: metric, **baseline** (the person's figure, or `[TBD]`), **target**, **date**
  (the end of the plan horizon unless the person gave one), and the funnel stage.
- A **north-star metric**: the one number that best shows the app delivering value to its users
  (for example weekly active loggers), and why.
- Smith's 5Ss (sell, serve, speak, save, sizzle) are a prompt for what objectives could cover;
  you don't need one of each.
- Targets are proposals for the person to approve. Never state a baseline you weren't given.

## S3 Strategy (PR Smith's TOPPP SEED, sharpened with Ritson and Rumelt)

Keep it to about one page. It must make choices.

- **Targets:** 1–3 segments from S1, the primary one first. Say why each (size, need, reach,
  fit with the app).
- **Objectives:** point back to S2.
- **Positioning:** one sentence: for <target>, <app> is the <frame> that <benefit>, because
  <reason to believe>. Then the **3Cs check** (Ritson): customers want it (evidence from S1),
  competitors don't own it (which ones come close), the company can deliver it (which features).
- **Partnerships, processes, sequence, engagement, experience, data** (the rest of TOPPP SEED):
  one line each, only where it changes what happens. Sequence matters most on a small budget:
  what comes first and why.
- **The obstacle** (Rumelt): the one real challenge the strategy must overcome, named plainly
  (for example "installs come from search but most new users leave in week one").
- **Ruled out:** segments, channels or messages the plan will not pursue this period, and why.
  A strategy that rules nothing out is not a strategy.
