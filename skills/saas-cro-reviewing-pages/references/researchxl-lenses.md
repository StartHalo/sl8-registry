# ResearchXL's five lenses, for B2B SaaS pages

Peep Laja's heuristic analysis (CXL, "Conversion research") walks each key page with five
questions. The answers are **areas of interest**, opinions to confirm with data later, not facts.
Judge from the visitor's side: a busy buyer at a small business or organisation, arriving with one
problem, deciding in seconds whether this is for them.

| Lens | The question | On a B2B SaaS page, look for |
|---|---|---|
| **Relevance** | Does the page match what the visitor came for? | Headline names the buyer's problem in their words, not the product's category; the page speaks to the buyer the site says it serves; ad or search promise matches the page |
| **Clarity** | Is it instantly clear what this is, who it's for and what to do next? | One main call to action per page; the headline says what the product does; plain words over jargon; the next step is named ("Book a 20-minute demo" beats "Get started") |
| **Value** | Why this, why now, why from you? | Concrete outcomes (time or money saved, risk removed); the price or how pricing works; what's included; proof next to the claim it supports |
| **Friction** | What makes the next step harder or riskier than it needs to be? | Form length and personal questions; "contact us" instead of a price; unclear what happens after submitting; no answer to the buyer's top fears (setup effort, switching, data security, contract length, who uses it) |
| **Distraction** | What pulls attention away from the one action? | Competing calls to action; leftover or off-topic content; navigation and pop-ups on the form page; several offers of equal weight |

## Page by page

- **Home:** relevance and clarity first. Can a buyer say in one sentence what this is and whether
  it's for them? Is there one obvious next step for the main path?
- **Pricing:** value and friction. Is there a price, or a clear way to get one? What's the ask on
  each plan? Are the plan differences meaningful to this buyer? Is proof or a guarantee near the
  decision?
- **Demo request:** friction. Count the fields; each one beyond name, work email and one
  qualifying question needs a reason. Say what happens next and how long it takes.
- **Trial sign-up:** friction. Card or no card, fields, what the first screen after sign-up asks.
  Map it with trial-path-map.md.

## Severity

Rate each finding 1–5 on the visitor's side:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Blocks or misleads most visitors on the main path (broken form, no way to see a price or book) |
| 4 | Costs many visitors on the main path (unclear what the product is; a long or invasive form) |
| 3 | Costs some visitors, or many on a secondary path |
| 2 | Weakens the case but rarely loses anyone alone |
| 1 | Polish |

A review where everything is a 3 has not been prioritised. One reviewer's severity is less
reliable than three reviewers' average; the review says so in its limits.
