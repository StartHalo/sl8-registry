# Mapping the trial path (Wes Bush, Bowling Alley framework)

For trial-led or freemium sites. Bush maps every step from sign-up to the outcome the customer
wanted, then labels each one:

| Label | Means | Usual fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | needed to reach the first useful result | keep; make it faster |
| **Yellow** | useful, but can wait until after first value | move it later |
| **Red** | not needed at all | remove it |

Bush's finding: a large share of required onboarding steps are unnecessary. The test for each
step is "does the buyer need this to see the product work for them?"

## How to map from outside the product

The bot never signs in, so it maps only what is public or supplied:

1. The sign-up button and page: fields (each one green, yellow or red), card required or not, what
   the page promises happens next.
2. Any public page describing setup ("Get started in 3 steps", help pages).
3. Screenshots or notes the founder supplies of the first screens after sign-up.

Write each step as `<step> — green|yellow|red|not seen — <why, with a quote>`. Steps inside the
product that weren't supplied are `not seen`, with a kit item asking the founder for screenshots
of the first three screens after sign-up.

The measures Bush ties to this path: activation (reaching first value), time to value, and free-to-paid
conversion. Name the ones the founder could track.
