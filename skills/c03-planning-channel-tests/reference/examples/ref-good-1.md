# Bullseye, and how Mint used it

**Rank every channel:** "Place each of the traction channels into one of three columns… Column A
(Inner Circle): which traction channels seem most promising right now? Column B (Potential)…
Column C (Long-shot)". Then "identify your inner circle: the three traction channels that seem
most promising."

**Test cheaply:** tests "should be designed to answer: Roughly how much will it cost to acquire
customers through this channel? How many customers do you think are available…? Are the customers
that you are getting… the ones that you want right now?" A test can give "a rough idea of a
channel's effectiveness with just a few hundred dollars". Weinberg (2015): "don't run a test longer
than a month or more than $1000 except in extenuating circumstances".

**Mint:** goal 100,000 users in six months. Inner circle: targeting blogs, PR, search engine
marketing. Cheap tests: "sponsored a small newsletter, reached out to financial celebrities…,
placed some Google ads". Focus: blogs brought the first 40,000; then PR.

## What makes it good (countable)
- Every channel goes into column A, B or C; the inner circle is three.
- Each test is capped (a month, $1,000) and answers the same three questions.
- The goal has a number and a date; the focus follows the test that worked.
