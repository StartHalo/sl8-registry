# Method: Bullseye, for a founder with a few hours a week

From Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares, *Traction* ch. 2
(https://leananalyticsbook.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/Traction-1-3.pdf).

1. **Consider every channel.** All 19 in [channels.md](channels.md), including ones the founder has
   never tried. For each, one idea of what it would look like for this product and this buyer.
2. **Rank each channel:**
   - **A (inner circle):** promising now: it reaches the primary segment, the price can pay for it,
     and it can be tested within the founder's hours and budget;
   - **B (potential):** could work later, with more budget, or after a winner is found;
   - **C (long shot):** unlikely for this buyer or price. One line of reason for every rank.
3. **Test from the A list, cheaply.** *Traction* tests three in parallel; a founder with a few hours
   a week runs **at most two at a time** (our narrowing), and starts the next when one ends. Each
   test runs at most about a month and $1,000 (Weinberg). Each answers three questions: roughly
   what a customer costs through it, how many customers it can reach, whether they are the ones
   wanted. A test uses its own channel's tactic, as [channels.md](channels.md) describes it: emails
   or calls to companies one by one are Sales, whoever they go to; Business development is a deal
   with one partner who brings many customers. A tactic whose channel is ranked B or C is not tested,
   and the reason given for that rank holds for every test. Each test reaches the ICP's primary
   segment; a test for another segment says why in its row.
4. **Set the threshold now:** one number deeper than clicks, with a window: trials, demos booked,
   replies from the right companies. A slow channel (search, content) uses a leading indicator it
   can show inside 13 weeks (first demo requests from the pages, pages ranking) and says so.
5. **Against the goal.** Add up what the thresholds would bring in the quarter, in the goal's unit
   (trials, demos), and set it against the goal over the same time. Say it plainly: the tests find
   the channel that can carry the goal; they are not the goal. Say what reaching the goal needs
   beyond them (the winner scaled from a named week, more budget, a later round).
6. **Standing actions.** Cheap, slow work that belongs in most B2B plans even when it is not a test:
   review-site profiles and a steady review ask, integration marketplace listings. Each with its
   hours a week and cost.
7. **The founder's week.** The tests running in a week plus the standing actions stay within the
   founder's hours; when the project has a launch brief, its actions in that week count too.
8. **Focus.** When a test clears its threshold, put the hours from the others into it until growth
   from it flattens; then run Bullseye again. A test that misses stops on its last week.
