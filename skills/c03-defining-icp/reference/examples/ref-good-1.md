# Userlist: April Dunford's method applied by a three-person bootstrapped B2B SaaS

Before: "email automation for SaaS"; the page says "our elevator pitch clearly didn't work".

## Competitive alternatives (as the page lists them)
- "Follow up with new customers manually"
- "Build an in-house solution"
- "Use a general-purpose email automation tool like Drip or ConvertKit"
- "User a dedicated messaging tool like Intercom or Customer.io"

## Value themes
- "Get started with less effort/resources"
- "Stay on top of their user situation (user management)"
- "Communicate successfully to increase adoption and retention"

## Best-fit customers
- "They're small (very few team members to help implement)"
- "They need to DIY (they either don't have much money, OR if they do, that money is going elsewhere)"
- "They have to pick & choose wisely where to spend their time"

## Market category
"Customer messaging tools" (changed from "email automation").

## Statement
"Onboard and engage your users with Userlist — a customer messaging tool for SaaS companies. More
efficient than building it yourself; less complex than Intercom or Customer.io."

**Evidence:** "6 months doing an enormous amount of demo calls" (count not given).

## What makes it good (countable)
- Four named alternatives, two of them "do it by hand" and "build it ourselves".
- Three value themes, each an action the customer takes.
- The category change and the "less complex than X" contrast fit in one sentence.
- Its best-fit customers have our persona's constraint: few people, little money, little time.

**Not shown:** unique attributes as a list, firmographic numbers, a trigger, named places to reach.
