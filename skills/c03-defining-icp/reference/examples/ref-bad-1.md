# A database startup positioned as "a database"

The company had a patented way to query a large amount of data quickly. It called itself a
database and competed against Oracle. In Dunford's words, "most folks wouldn't even take a meeting
with us". After repositioning it as a Business Intelligence tool for banks, security companies and
online advertising firms, the value read as answering questions "while a customer is on the phone
instead of having to call them back the next day".

## Why the "before" is bad (against the card)
- **Alternatives:** the wrong yardstick. Oracle is what the category implies, not what buyers with
  the problem used.
- **Category:** "database" hid the one attribute that mattered.
- **Segment:** none. No company type, size, buyer, trigger or place to reach.
- **Proof:** none.

A second "before" of the same kind: Userlist's "email automation for SaaS" (good-1), which sent
buyers to compare it with general email tools.
