# Firefox for Android "Camera" holiday campaign brief

A real, public brief for a mobile app feature launch: Firefox for Android's new Camera UI, which let web pages use the phone's camera. It is useful because of the specific ways it falls short.

## Objective
"Increase awareness of Firefox for Android and drive engagement". The background also says the aim is "to acquire Firefox for Android users" and to introduce Firefox to "a more mainstream audience".

## Proposition
Two value propositions, one for each audience:
- Mainstream user: "Use innovative Web technologies to transform every day pictures into fun and artsy photos to share"
- Developer: "Firefox and the Web is a compelling platform to build for"

## Reasons to believe
— (the brief describes the feature: take photos in the browser, add filters and effects, share via social networks and email, with no separate camera app needed. It gives no proof points.)

## Audience
Two segments: "Compatible Android device owners who use photo-sharing apps" and "Firefox desktop users who own compatible devices". The background also mentions current developer and tech-enthusiast users.

## KPI
Metrics are named with no targets: P1 "photos shared, download conversions"; P2 "campaign impressions, CTR to /mobile, bounce rate". The only dates are a campaign launch on Feb 7 and an end date of "Late Mar - EOL?".

## Channels
About ten: online ads (Facebook, blogs such as Mashable, TechCrunch, Huffington Post and Lifehacker, plus Google, Yahoo, Bing), mobile ads, press, mozilla.org promotions, social media, snippets, community, and student reps. A mobile-optimised microsite is at the centre.

## Budget
—

## Deliverables
Only implied: the microsite (capture, decorate and share photos) and ads. The page ends with a brainstorm of campaign names ("Foxography", "Shutterfox", "Firefox Snap" and others).

## Out of scope
—

## Why it is bad
- Its objective covers three things at once: awareness, engagement and acquisition. The brief never says which one wins.
- It is not single-minded. It has two propositions for two audiences (consumers and developers), so the agency would effectively get two briefs.
- The KPIs are a list of metrics with no number or deadline, so success cannot be judged. "Late Mar - EOL?" is a question, not an end date.
- About ten channels, no budget and no deliverables list mean nothing is prioritised. This is the "when you try to be everywhere, you might end up nowhere" failure that Beloved Brands warns about.
- There is no proof for the claim ("innovative", "fun and artsy") and nothing is ruled out of scope.
