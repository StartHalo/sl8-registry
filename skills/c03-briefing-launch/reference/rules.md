# Rules for the launch brief

1. **One proposition, one sentence, one buyer.** Two audiences make two briefs; choose one.
2. **Reasons to believe are facts,** each proving the same outcome, true on launch day, and ending
   with its source's exact words: `(source: <where>, "<the words>")`. With fewer facts, give fewer
   and say what to send; never invent one.
3. **The KPI is use by a date,** not sign-ups or impressions alone, with a guardrail.
4. **Every action has a week from launch day, its date, an owner, the founder's hours and a cost.**
   Someone else's work costs the founder 0 hours, or the hours to brief them.
5. **Hours and money add up:** the founder's hours in any week stay within the stated hours, with
   the channel tests' hours in that week added when the project has a channel-test plan; the
   budget lines sum to the total, the actions cost the same total, within the budget. When the
   two plans cannot fit the founder's week, the brief assumes the extra hours openly (header marked
   `(assumed)`, an Assumptions line, a decision for the founder), never silently.
6. **The partner's lead times come first:** a marketplace listing or partner email is asked for in
   the weeks before, with the date it is due, from the partner's pages you read.
7. **Every claim has a source:** a page page.mjs saved, the request, or an earlier deliverable of
   the project. Quotation marks hold a source's exact words; titles and phrases you propose are in
   italics. A count, a size or a place no source gives is left out or goes under Assumptions.
8. **A brief is not a plan.** This launch only; it does not repeat the channel tests.
9. **One page plus the actions table.**
