# S4 channel choice: Bullseye, for a founder with few hours

From Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares (*Traction*), with Rob Walling's "master one or two
channels before adding more".

1. **Consider every channel** on the list in [channels.md](channels.md), including ones the
   founder hasn't tried. For each, one idea of what it would look like for this product, and how
   similar products found customers there.
2. **Rank** each channel:
   - **A:** promising now: reaches the target segment, fits the price (channel-model fit, S3),
     can be tested within the founder's hours.
   - **B:** could work later or with more budget.
   - **C:** long shot, or ruled out in S3. Say why.
3. **Pick at most 3 to test**, from the A list, where excitement drops off. Channels in use stay
   only if the ranking keeps them.
4. **Design each test cheaply**, before any spend:
   - what to do, for how long (2–6 weeks);
   - the cost and the hours a week;
   - the threshold, set now: what result, by what date, means "this channel works" (for example
     "at least 3 demo requests that came from the review site in 6 weeks");
   - the three questions each test answers: what a customer costs through it, how many customers
     it can bring, and whether they are the customers wanted.
5. **Focus:** after the tests, put most hours into the one channel that cleared its threshold,
   until it stops growing. Say when the tests end and when to run Bullseye again (when growth
   from the focus channel flattens).

At most 3 test channels. A founder with 5 hours a week can rarely run more than 2 at once:
stagger them if needed and say so.

## Review sites and marketplaces

When buyers compare software before a demo, these are cheap and slow to build, so they belong in
most plans even if not a test channel:
- claim and complete the profiles on the review sites buyers in this category use;
- a steady review ask: to customers at a good moment (after a success, at renewal), with the
  founder's draft email;
- integration marketplace listings for tools the product connects to, with the partner's
  requirements noted.

## Pricing message

How to present the current price so it reads as worth it to the target segment: the unit buyers
think in (per site, per user, per year), the comparison to the main alternative's cost, what's
included. A recommended price change goes under `## Open decisions` as the founder's decision,
never as done.
