# Guidelines a small team uses

Wheeler's phase 5 manages the brand's assets: launch, brand champions, an online home and
guidelines. Her 6th edition records the shift from rigid, static PDF rules approved at the end
to "education, empowerment, and self-service", with tailored content for different users. For a
small app team that means a short, living brand book with examples, an owner, and templates.

## What makes guidelines usable

- **Rules need examples.** A do and a don't, side by side, beat a paragraph of restrictions.
  Avoid undefined words ("professional", "clean").
- **Say when and why** a rule applies, so people can handle cases it doesn't cover.
- **Templates help people create what guidelines describe.** Give fill-in structures for the
  things the team writes every week.
- **An owner and a home.** Guidelines work only if people know where they live, who owns them
  and how they change.
- **Living, not finished.** Reviewed on a cadence, and earlier when something triggers it.

## Do and don't pairs

| Area | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Voice | the on-voice example from S3 | the off-voice example |
| Messages | lead with the umbrella message | stack three messages in one post |
| Colour | the brand colour on the stated background | the accent as body text if it fails contrast |
| Type | the display face for headlines only | more than two families |
| Imagery | the imagery rule from S3 | the category cliché from S1 |
| Health claims | the safer wording | the flagged claim |

## Consistency checklist (Neumeier's tests, plus ours)

- **Focus test:** does this piece say who we are, what we do and why it matters, in a breath?
- **Hand test:** cover the app's name. Can you still tell it's ours from the words and look?
- **Swap test:** put a competitor's name on it. If it works as well, it isn't ours yet.
- Does it use one message, in our voice, with our palette and type?
- Does it pass the health-claims check?

## Review cadence and triggers

At least a yearly review of the whole book; the touchpoints and templates quarterly. Refresh early
on a launch, a new audience or market, a repeated question from the team, or a review that finds
drift. A review is the bot's Review job; a refresh is Build our brand: refresh.
