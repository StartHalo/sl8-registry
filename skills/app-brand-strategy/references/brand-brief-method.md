# Brand brief method

Wheeler's phase 2 (clarifying strategy) narrows the focus, positions the brand and writes a brand
brief. The tools below come from the practitioners Wheeler herself draws on; each is credited.

## Strategy is choosing (Ritson)

Three questions: who are we targeting, what do we want to stand for, how will we get there.
Strategy is deciding what not to do, so every brief has a "we will not" list. Diagnose first:
every choice here points back to a finding in S1.

## Positioning inputs (April Dunford)

Work through them in order; each feeds the next.

1. **Competitive alternatives:** what people would use if this app didn't exist (other apps, a
   notebook, a habit, doing nothing). Take them from S1's competitors and review themes.
2. **Unique attributes:** what this app has that the alternatives don't (a feature, an approach,
   a data practice, a tone).
3. **Value:** what those attributes let people do or feel, in their own words from the reviews.
4. **Best-fit audience:** who cares most about that value.
5. **Market frame:** the category that makes the value obvious ("a sleep journal", not "a
   wellness platform").

Then one statement: "For <audience> who <need>, <app> is the <frame> that <value>, unlike
<alternative>, because <attribute>."

## Onliness (Marty Neumeier)

"Our brand is the only ___ that ___." If it's hard to finish, the positioning isn't sharp
enough. The check: name each competitor from S1 and ask whether it could truthfully say the same
sentence. If yes, the statement isn't different yet.

## Brand vision (David Aaker)

- **Core values (2–5):** what the brand stands for, most important first. Each needs a **proof
  point**: something the app already does that shows it (a feature, a policy, a review theme).
  A value with no proof is an aspiration; label it so.
- **Extended identity:** the personality traits that give texture (they feed S3's voice).
- **Essence:** 2–4 words that hold the core together. Internal, not a tagline.

## The promise

What a person can count on every time they meet the brand. One sentence, in plain words, inside
the health-claims limits. Good: "Five calm minutes, whenever you need them." Bad: "Transform
your wellbeing."

## Quality checks before writing

- Could a competitor say it? Then it's not positioning.
- Does every value have a proof point, or an honest label?
- Does "we will not" rule out something tempting (a cliché competitors use, a promise the app
  can't keep)?
- Is anything a health claim? Sort it with the health-claims check.
