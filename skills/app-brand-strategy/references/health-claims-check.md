# Health-claims check

A wellness app's copy often makes health claims without meaning to ("reduces stress", "sleep
better"). App stores and advertising law limit them. This check **flags points for a person to
verify**. It never states that copy is compliant and gives no legal or medical sign-off.

## Sort every claim into one of three groups

| Group | What it sounds like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Everyday wellness** | describes what the app helps the person do, with no promised health result: "a calmer evening routine", "log how you slept", "reminders to stretch" | usually fine; keep it concrete |
| **Needs evidence** | promises a measurable health result, or uses numbers or science words: "reduces stress", "improves sleep by 30%", "science-backed", "clinically tested" | flag it: the claim needs evidence the company holds, or soften it to the everyday-wellness form |
| **Medical** | diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, predict a medical condition, or replace a doctor: "detects anxiety", "treats insomnia", "predicts migraines" | flag it as high risk: only for an app regulated as a medical device; otherwise rewrite |

## Store and platform rules to name when flagging

- **Apple App Review Guideline 1.4.1** (physical harm, medical apps): apps that claim medical
  accuracy or diagnosis get closer review and must disclose methodology; claims must be
  validated.
- **Apple Guideline 5.1.1 and 5.1.3** (health data): health data can't be used for advertising
  or shared with third parties for it; privacy claims must match what the app does.
- **Google Play Health Content and Services policy**: an app that isn't a regulated medical
  device says so ("not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any
  medical condition") where health features could suggest otherwise.
- **Advertising law** in most markets needs evidence for health claims in ads and store copy.

Name the rule; don't quote it as settled law. Policy pages change: say "check the current
guideline".

## Privacy and data promises

A promise about data ("we never sell your data", "private by design") is a claim too. Flag it
to verify against the app's actual data practice and privacy policy.

## How to write the check

```text
## Health-claims check
| Claim (where) | Group | Why it's flagged | Rule to check | Safer wording |
|---|---|---|---|---|
```

End with one line: "This flags what to verify. It is not legal or medical sign-off."
