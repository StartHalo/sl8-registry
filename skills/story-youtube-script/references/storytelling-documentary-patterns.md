## Hook types (ten)

| # | Type | Mechanics | Invented shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Prize and preview | Premise, prize and rules inside about 15 s, then a ladder of what is coming. | "Two hundred strangers, one maze, and the final person standing keeps 250,000." |
| H2 | Dated scene | Exact date and place, an ordinary present-tense scene, one planted wrong detail, a stakes line. | "4 November 2017, just after nine. A van pulls up at a quiet depot. Its plates are fake." |
| H3 | Misconception reversal | The belief, its reversal, and how far off the belief is. | "Ports do not earn their money from cargo." |
| H4 | Record object | The extreme thing, two or three escalating comparisons, then the how. | "This safe has never been opened. Tonight it will be." |
| H5 | Investigator's obsession | The narrator's long fixation and a promise to show what turned up. | "Two years of dead ends, and finally a name." |
| H6 | Archive collage | Real broadcast or audio first; the narrator frames it. | Narration laid over old news tape. |
| H7 | Shadow of the ending | Reveal the ending's outline early, without its cause. | "None of them knows the ship will be gone by spring." |
| H8 | Impossible narrator | A comic persona owns the retelling: an unlikely narrator, a promise to tell the story, a hard date. | The last passenger on a sinking liner tells it from the lifeboat. Never a real person's persona or voice. |
| H9 | What-if ladder | A playful question answered rung by rung from the smallest step. | "How tall could a tower really get?" |
| H10 | Broken explanation | The accepted answer, at once rejected as not enough. | "The weather, people said. It was not the weather." |

Dated scenes and record objects account for about half the openings; misconception reversals lead
in the geography and scale channels. Very few open with a jingle, a self-introduction or a long
"in today's video" line.

## Act structure

- **0-2 Hook.** The title's promise said again, almost word for word, inside 30 s.
- **2-8 Frame and standing.** Size the mystery and the stakes, and say what the video will show;
  investigative channels credit their sources here or ask the viewer to hold judgement to the
  end.
- **8-15 Back to the start.** After a near-climax opening, time jumps back; the opening is a loan
  the middle repays.
- **25-35 First break.** The story reaches a stable plateau and pays out part of its promise, then
  an explicit re-hook returns to the night it all went wrong.
- **35-70 Escalation.** Information comes out a chapter at a time, and every chapter closes with the stakes higher. The answer stays
  withheld through this zone; identity, cause and verdict come in parts (for example a handle at
  about 45, a name at about 55, a country at about 65).
- **70-85 Convergence.** The opening scene returns with full context; challenge formats reach the
  last two; mysteries turn from chronology to eliminating theories.
- **85-100 Verdict and wider view.** The narrator takes a hedged position (a ranking of the most
  plausible theories; "I found nothing that proves it"), then widens to a general truth or a
  closing maxim. Challenge formats end seconds after the winner reacts.

Two deviations: company sagas put a "why you should care" essay of several minutes before the
chronology; economic essays skip the narrative opening (thesis, proof, history, mechanism,
counterpoint, maxim).

## Retention devices

- **Promissory notes:** short and always repaid: name the thing, defer the mechanism, repay within
  2-5 minutes. The ending's shadow (H7) is the longest-running loop because it covers the whole
  video.
- **Refrains:** the same unanswered questions repeated word for word about three times, each one reopening the question once part of it has been answered; or a detail the viewer is told to keep in mind
  because it decides the ending; or the prize restated every couple of minutes.
- **Counters:** players remaining, days, a shrinking arena, depth or size; progress made numeric
  and the next milestone always visible.
- **Stakes on a schedule:** a rule change each act; the founder's "biggest bet yet"; scope widening
  step by step (a courier, then the organiser, then a network across countries, then the sting);
  comic sagas where each incident is worse than the last.
- **Interrupts:** a physical stunt in the middle of an essay, skits every 60-90 s, contestant
  interviews, a puzzle for the viewer; they appear once exposition runs a minute or more.
- **Micro-delays:** a recovered handle read one character at a time; a mid-reveal double-take.
- **Trust as retention:** the hedge arrives at the biggest claim (nobody knew the outcome in
  advance; that part could not be verified; warnings before quoting a figure; debunking your own
  best evidence). The sceptical aside earns belief for the next claim.

## Payoff rhythm

A payoff is a reveal, an elimination, a punchline or a finished mini-story.

| Lane | Payoff interval |
|---|---|
| Scale ladder | 10-20 s: one fact plus one physical comparison per rung |
| Game or challenge | 20-40 s; the rules or format change about each 2-3 minutes |
| Comic retelling | jokes 5-15 s apart, laid over plot movement each 60-90 s |
| Economic essay | a statistic with a punchline every 30-60 s |
| Procedural or forensic mystery | each chapter pays off within 2-4 minutes; two or three big reveals near 45, 65 and 80 percent |
| Long saga or forum investigation (30-80 min) | chapters 3-5 minutes long, each closing on a small cliffhanger |

Two spacing rules: no set-up waits longer than about a third of the video (the opening scene is the
one exception, repaid at 70-85); payoffs are densest in minutes one to three and just after each
act break, since both are points where viewers re-enter.

## Quick card

- **Hooks (30 s or less):** prize and preview; dated scene with the shadow of the ending;
  misconception reversal; record object; investigator's obsession.
- **Beats:** 0-2 hook and the title promise again → 2-8 frame, promise, sources → 8-15 back to the
  start → 25-35 plateau and act break → 35-70 three to five unsettled chapters, staged reveals, one
  twist each → 70-85 the opening scene completed, the biggest reveal → 85-100 hedged verdict, a
  general truth or maxim, any call to action last.
- **Rules:** present tense inside scenes, past for analysis; short punches between long
  explanations; a comparison for every figure, a gloss for every term, a role for every person;
  promises planted early and all repaid, one long loop at most; a visible counter; one caveat on
  the biggest claim; chapters end unsettled; stop seconds after the emotional peak; the core
  question repeated three times.
