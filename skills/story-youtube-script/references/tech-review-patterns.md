# Tech review patterns

Optional depth for [tech-review.md](tech-review.md); read only when it helps. Example shapes are

## Hook types (nine)

| # | Type | Mechanics | Invented shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Spend stunt | Three tooled sentences: a first-person commitment with two figures; a widening of scope; a test promise posed as a stakes question. A past-tense variant reports the purchase done, adds a fairness set-up, then asks the question. | "I have ten minutes and 18,000 of my own dollars for the most absurd gadgets online. Then we find out which ones are real." |
| H2 | Fresh dispatch | Recency stamp, proof of labour (all of the pages), and a promise to keep only what matters. A comic variant opens on the irony of the news. | "A 90-page report landed this morning. I read every page; six findings matter." |
| H3 | Verdict first | Rule at once, then pose the deeper question; suspense moves from "is it good?" to "why?". | "Easy recommendation. The interesting part is why the cheaper one is nearly as good." |
| H4 | Disaster open | The video starts inside a failure, then promises to find out how it happened. | "One toggle, and the phone is a brick." |
| H5 | Refused object | Access is the story: the maker would not sell, fix or send it. | "They would not repair it. So we opened it ourselves." |
| H6 | Countdown | A ranked list whose top entry is teased, never named early. | "One of these breaks our scoring scale." |
| H7 | Contrarian | State the consensus, refuse it. | "Every list says learn this first. Not today." |
| H8 | Character sketch | Introduce an entity in two set-up lines; the twist reveals the thesis. | The gag set-up is secretly the thesis. |
| H9 | Results reel | Rapid finished outputs first, then the promise of the exact steps. | Proof before the promise (tutorials). |

## Beat maps

**One product, typically 15-25 minutes long:**
- 0-5 percent: hook, standing ("a short hands-on before launch"), the coverage promise.
- 5-12: what it is; the price early only when the price is the story.
- 12-70: walkthrough in one of three orders, never the spec sheet's:
  1. *curiosity order*: the questions a newcomer asks, in sequence (what it is, how it works, what
     it can do, whether it is good); sections can carry those questions as titles;
  2. *physical tour*: follow the object part by part, so the camera path is the outline;
  3. *use order*: unbox, set up, first start, real task (generational gauntlets go through time).
  Whatever the order, the most impressive feature moves forward and is named as such.
- 70-85: downsides, counted aloud.
- 85-95: the ruling with pros and cons weighed, the buyer it suits, and a scope hedge.
- 95-100: a sign-off pointing to the next video.

**Lists and gauntlets:** hook → the sort rule stated at once (cheapest to dearest, tiers by depth)
→ per item: set-up, demo, score → overall ruling. Position signals escalation, so the sort is the
spine.

**News analysis:** recency hook → a 30-second primer → counted findings, biggest first or held back as a tease → what they mean → sign-off. Each finding gets an immediate judgement, and a closing
paragraph calibrates the whole.

**Where the verdict sits:** single-product reviewers at about 85-90 percent; per-item scorers make
the final ranking the verdict; dispatch analysts rule at the top and then defend; satirists use a
closing punchline.

## Retention devices

- **Named deferral:** raise a feature-specific question, promise it later, and use the move many
  times in one script.
- **Catch turn:** praise, then a twist that complicates it. Whole videos can alternate competence
  and blunder.
- **Running tally:** a recurring gag (an off-screen voice answering "what did this part cost?")
  turns a parts list into a game with a final total; a live spend counter does the same.
- **Ranking tension:** scores per item invite argument; the sort order promises better is coming;
  the extreme entry is teased early.
- **Held reveals:** the total price as the last beat; a fake's identity held until tests force it;
  the most startling finding flagged as still to come.
- **Delayed title stamp:** a recurring date-and-show ritual lands a minute or so in, after the hook,
  giving a built-in break. Any mid-video subscribe line plays off what is on screen.
- **Loop closing:** the hook plants a question the ending answers in the same words.

## Opinion architecture

- **Own the take:** opinions carry a marker ("I think", "for me"), and the host admits where their
  expertise runs out.
- **Show, then judge:** demonstration always comes before the ruling; benchmark against a control
  before condemning; put the original document's words on screen before ruling.
- **Three hedges:** time (ask again in a year), sample (only the unit we received), knowledge (not
  verified yet; priors stated and updated on camera).
- **Negativity styles:** show the flaw hurting a real user, concede to critics in advance; comic
  outrage only at objective failures, always with a fairness line; disappointment made legible by
  a score; irony that dresses the criticism as praise; criticism aimed at claims, paired with the
  counter-evidence.
- **Escape valve:** a harsh ruling closes on the buyer it still suits, or the change that would
  redeem it.

## Density at high speed

Two styles for 190-235 words a minute.

**Comic density** (one measured script: roughly a laugh each 30 words, so six to eight a minute,
still about 15 factual beats in under four minutes):
- jokes carry the information, never pure asides;
- allusion compresses: one well-known reference replaces a paragraph;
- recurring rituals (a date sting, a fixed sign-off) tell the viewer where they are;
- open a topic with a sarcastic statement of the common mistake, then give the real content;
- list scaffolds remove the need for long transitions; each tier opens with a short theme sentence;
- a silly or philosophical coda releases the pressure at the end.

**Evidence density** (claims a minute, each with its source):
- numbered revelations, each self-contained and skippable;
- proof of labour buys the right to summarise hard;
- order by impact with one visible, flagged exception;
- a large claim has its limit beside it, within the next sentence or so, so no caveats block is
  needed;
- heavy signposting, one idea a sentence, recurring anchor metaphors.

At these speeds syntax stays flat: subject, verb, object; no nested clauses; anything hard is paired with an analogy almost at once (inside five seconds).

## Quick card

- **Hook:** a type from the guide, at most 3 sentences and 15 seconds, with a number and an effort
  verb.
- **One product:** 0-5 hook, standing, coverage; 5-12 frame, price only if it is news; 12-70
  curiosity or tour order, best feature early, three or more named deferrals; 70-85 counted
  downsides; 85-95 ruling, buyer, hedge; 95-100 themed sign-off and next video.
- **List:** announce the ordering in the second sentence → set-up, demo, score each → tally → overall ruling and
  total.
- **Retention:** a deferral each 2 minutes; one catch turn a section; the biggest figure in the
  last fifth, hinted once early.
- **Register:** first person, present, contractions; on camera 160-200 wpm, voiceover
  200-235; flat
  syntax; specs as meanings, multiples or overlays.
- **Opinion:** demo first; owned takes; a fairness line and an escape valve for every criticism; a
  comparison or a check for every hype claim.
- **Close:** nothing before the hook or inside a test; one subscribe line at most, fitted to the moment and naming what is coming; sign off within seconds of the ruling.
- **Why it works:** the host does the work the viewer will not (spend, break, benchmark, read) and
  reports back, sceptical until the product changes their mind on camera.
