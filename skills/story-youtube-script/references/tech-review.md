## Intake

Take what the brief gives; fill the rest with the defaults below and list them in the delivery
(HR12: no question tool).

1. **Format.**
   - **One product, reviewed or first impressions** (on camera, 10-25 min).
   - **Gauntlet or ranking**: several products, a verdict on each.
   - **News analysis or roundup**: dense voiceover, driven by what is new.
   - **Tutorial or demo**: narration over a screen recording.
   Default: one-product review when one product is named and labor proof exists; news analysis
   when there is no labor proof; a kit or brief may name another.
2. **Labor proof.** What the creator really did or will do: money spent, days of use, pages read,
   a build, a breakage. A hands-on hook needs a figure and a true first-person effort verb. With
   no labor proof, never write a hands-on review: switch to the sourced news-analysis or tutorial
   lane, which says out loud that nothing was tested (path P3).
3. **The real verdict.** What the creator actually thinks. Write the review backwards from it.
   Without one, the hands-on lane is closed (a verdict cannot be invented); the sourced lane
   reports what the named sources found instead.
4. **Scope hedge.** What was not tested; the verdict must say it.
5. **Length and pace.** On-camera formats run 160-200 words a minute, dense voiceover 200-235.
   Default length: 10 minutes. At 4 minutes or less, use Short-form mode.

## Hooks

Three options, each at most three sentences and 15 seconds. Hands-on hooks carry a number and a
true effort verb (spent, read, bought, broke, tested). A sourced lane uses a named evidence action
("the filing says", "the report shows") and never implies a test.

| Type | Shape (our wording) |
|---|---|
| Spend stunt | The host commits a stated sum within a stated time on the most extreme item of a category, then promises to test whether it is a dream or a con. |
| Fresh dispatch | A time stamp for the event, proof that the host went through all of it, and a count of the points that matter. |
| Verdict up front | The one-line ruling first, then the deeper "why" becomes the mystery. |
| Disaster open | A failure plays live; the host did everything right; what happened? |
| Refused object | A gatekeeper would not sell, fix or ship it; the host got one anyway. |
| Countdown | N best or worst, with the top spot teased and never named early. |
| Contrarian | The common advice is stated, then rejected for today's video. |
| Results reel | A fast run of finished results, then a promise to show the exact steps (tutorials). |

## Structure by format

**One product (percent of runtime):**
- **0-5:** hook, why the host can speak to it ("a week of daily use"), and what the video covers.
- **5-12:** what the thing is. Say the price here only if the price is the story; otherwise keep it back.
- **12-70:** the walkthrough, never in spec-sheet order. Use one of: the order a curious buyer's
  questions come in; a tour of the object from one end to the other; or the order of use (unbox,
  set up, do a real task). Move the best feature early and tell the viewer it is the best.
  Plant at least three named deferrals ("the battery comes later").
- **70-85:** the downsides, numbered aloud.
- **85-95:** the ruling: overall judgement, the buyer it suits, and what went untested.
- **95-100:** a short sign-off line and the next video, named.

**Gauntlet or ranking:** state the sort rule in the second sentence (price rising, countdown,
tiers); the announced order is what keeps viewers. Per item: set-up, live demo, score or verdict.
Keep a running total (money spent, points) and reveal it in the final beat.

**News analysis:** a hook on how new it is → a half-minute primer → findings by number, either
leading with the biggest or holding the wildest back and flagging it → a ruling on each finding with its caveat beside it
(no separate limitations block) → a closing paragraph that weighs it all → sign-off.

**Tutorial or demo:** the method records no map of its own (learnings STO-L428). Use the
one-product map with the walkthrough in order of use, and a results reel hook.

## Register and opinion

- First person, present tense, contractions throughout; never open a sentence in the passive.
- Alternate a long explaining sentence with a short reaction. At voiceover speed keep syntax flat:
  one idea a sentence, and an analogy within five seconds of anything hard.
- Never recite specs. Translate them ("which means ..."), turn them into multiples, or put the
  figures on screen while the voice says what they mean.
- Default to doubt; excitement is a change of mind the script shows happening.
- Show before judging. Mark opinions as yours ("I think", "for me"), and say where your knowledge
  stops.
- Every criticism gets a fairness line and a way out (who it still suits, what would fix it).
  Aim criticism at choices the maker made, never at owners.
- Any hype claim gets a comparison or a check; a maker's claim gets tested, or the script says it
  could not be.
- Hedge at three levels: time ("we'll see"), sample ("one unit, one week"), knowledge ("I can't
  confirm that yet").

## Retention

- A named deferral about every 2 minutes, always a specific feature.
- Call back only to deferrals this script really planted; a false "as I promised" breaks trust.
- One turn per section where praise meets a complication ("but ...").
- Save the largest figure (what it all cost, the winner, the most startling finding) for the last
  fifth; hint at it once early.
- Answer the hook's question at the close, echoing its words.
- Cheap serial reveals: a running price gag, a repeated poll, a score per item viewers can argue
  with.

## Ending

No invented sponsor, brand integration or plug; a specific one is included only when the user asks,
and never inside a test, demo or reveal that is under way. Otherwise finish with a sign-off line
of the host's own (never one a real creator uses) or a hook into what comes next. A subscribe line, if wanted, is one line, fits the
moment, and names a coming payoff; stock "smash the like button" lines are out.

## Anti-patterns

- Walking the spec sheet in order; invented monetization.
- Opinions nobody owns ("people say"); replace with what the host tested, read or bought.
- Superlatives with no comparison or demo.
- The verdict at 0:00 with no fresh "why" to follow it.
- Press-release language, unless quoted to check it or mock it.
- Apologising for length instead of earning it with density.
- Generic calls to action in the middle.

## Short-form mode

At 4 minutes or less, drop the long-form map; keep labor proof, translated specs, earned hype and
fairness lines.
- About 160-200 words a minute: 3 minutes is about 500-580 spoken words.
- The default hook is the verdict: ruling, number and effort verb in the first sentence.
- Map: verdict hook (0-10) → three proofs, best first (10-70) → the single drawback (70-85) →
  one line on who it suits and what was untested, then stop.
- No named deferrals; one figure (price or score) may be held back and given in the final line.
- Timestamps 15-30 seconds apart; a 3-5 line retention map; one outro line tied to a payoff,
  or no outro at all.
- Vertical Shorts: the product is on screen doing something within 2 seconds, the hook works
  without sound, and a loop-close is worth trying.

## Output format

```
# <Working title> - <format>, ~<N> min

## Hook options (pick one)
### Hook 1 - <type>
### Hook 2 - <type>
### Hook 3 - <type>
(each three sentences or fewer, with a number and an effort verb or a named evidence action)

## Script
[0:00] spoken text; short reactions set apart
[DEMO: ...] [B-ROLL: ...] [OVERLAY: spec figures] [SCORE: 8 of 10]

## Retention map
[m:ss] a line for each named deferral, catch turn, held reveal or tally beat
```

Titles and headings never grade the script's own pacing or rule keeping; the retention map names
devices, not grades.
