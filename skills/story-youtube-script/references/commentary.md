## Intake

Infer what the brief allows; fill gaps with the defaults below and name them in the delivery
(HR12: no question tool).

1. **The artifact.** The specific thing being judged (a clip, a movie, a trend, a maker, a gadget, a painting). Without one, commentary cannot exist. A vague topic ("awkward wedding videos") becomes
   two or three proposed artifacts, the script uses the first, and the delivery lists the others.
   Never describe footage the user has not supplied or a source does not record.
2. **Lane**, each with its own beat map:
   - **Comic commentary** (15-30 min): the host reacts to found footage or a phenomenon with jokes;
     teasing with affection is the goal.
   - **Deadpan mock-explainer** (2-8 min): a straight-faced guide in a calm, textbook voice; topic
     comedy without footage.
   - **Sincere video essay** (6-10 min): close analytical reading of one work; insight is the goal,
     not laughs.
   Default: the essay when the artifact is a single work and the brief asks why it works; comic
   commentary when footage is supplied; a kit or brief may name another.
3. **Length.** Minutes at about 140-150 spoken words a minute. Default: the shortest typical length
   of the lane (15, 2 or 6 min). At 4 minutes or less, use Short-form mode.
4. **Position.** What the host really thinks of the artifact; it must be heard before
   0:30. When the user gives no stance, the brief's own framing is the stance and the delivery says so.
5. **Persona facts** (optional): material for self-mockery, running bits, the audience's nickname.
   Use only what the user gives; never borrow a real creator's.

## Hooks

Three options from different rows, fitted to the artifact. Before 0:30 the viewer can name the
thing under discussion and where the host stands on it; there is no general warm-up.

| Type | Shape (our wording) | Lane |
|---|---|---|
| Sketch open | 10-20 s of an acted scene or character that stages the topic sideways, then a snap back to the normal voice. | Comic |
| Artifact first | About 10 s of the odd thing itself, raw; then the reaction; then "is it just me?" | All (essay default) |
| How it found me | Viewers kept sending the thing until the host finally dug in. | Comic |
| Costly experiment | The host commits to something expensive or irreversible to settle a question. | Experiment premise |
| Ruling first | The verdict (bad or brilliant) in one line, then the promise to earn it. | Comic, essay |
| Deadpan definition | Define some ordinary thing in a dry reference-book voice, with a straight face; undercut it
before 20 s. | Mock-explainer |
| Fake help | A guide offered to people who struggle with something, which then delivers anti-help. | Mock-explainer |
| Superlative object | Of a whole category, this one item is the most extreme; then count something concrete straight away. | Essay |
| Confession | An embarrassing true fact about the host that qualifies them to talk about the topic (only one the user supplied). | Mock-explainer, comic |

## Structure by lane

**Comic commentary (percent of runtime):**
- **0-3:** sketch open or greeting ritual.
- **3-10:** how the artifact reached the host, and the promise of today's premise. Any housekeeping
  is 15 s or less, self-aware, and cut short.
- **About 10:** a spoken chapter turn ("Right. Let's open it up.").
- **10-80:** the clip-react cycle, 30-90 s each:
  1. at most 20 s of the source material; footage never runs longer uninterrupted;
  2. say plainly what just happened;
  3. question its logic out loud, putting the viewer's own doubts to it;
  4. act it out: voice the hidden subtext as dialogue, or blow it up into an imagined scene;
  5. a tag line, then the next clip.
  Order segments from least to most absurd and join them with spoken escalation markers ("and it
  gets stranger").
- **40-60:** one mid-video turn: something that really went wrong becomes part of the plot, the
  thesis deepens, or the format twists.
- **80-90:** the verdict and one sincere paragraph on why it matters. Irony never has the last word.
- **90-100:** a light outro in persona (a subscribe gag that feeds a running bit, a joke tease of
  the next video), under 20 s.

**Deadpan mock-explainer:** definition → a scaffold announced up front (levels or stages) → a dozen to eighteen set-up-and-twist units
of roughly 15-25 s each → the top tier turns back on the speaker or the viewer →
a dead stop with no outro.

**Sincere essay:** the artifact cold → a single tentative "I think" thesis before the 15 percent mark → an evidence ladder: (1) count
something concrete (words, shots, seconds of silence); (2) give the device its proper term and a
quick gloss; (3) back it with an outside source; (4) interpret last → play the key moment again so
viewers notice how differently they now see it → in the final fifth, open out to what it means for
people → a closing line to "you" → a warm outro.

## Register (all lanes)

- Talk like a person, in the first person and the present, with contractions; clips are narrated
  as if happening now ("so then he turns round and ...").
- A peer, never a teacher. Hedge big claims through yourself ("I think", "for me"); comic precision
  is only for obviously invented hypotheticals. Every real figure still needs a source.
- Act confused before resolving; interrogate the artifact aloud.
- Self-mockery in three set places: the hook, the moment before the host mocks anyone else (it earns
  the right), and inside the call to action.
- Before mocking: check the harm is real, echo the people affected, and aim at institutions, output
  or behaviour; never at looks or at fans.
- Leave the seams showing: slips and production trouble become material.
- Build in three steps: the literal contradiction → an imagined scene → a world-sized consequence.
- Plant the oddest line early, bring it back unannounced two or three times, and name the last
  return (naming it is the joke).
- Call back only to what this script planted. Check the earlier line exists before any "like I said"
  or "callback!".

**Joke density:** mock-explainer: a reversal every 15-25 s, the twist as the last words of a calm
sentence, never signalled; comic commentary: a big laugh every 30-60 s, with small tags and acted bits filling the gaps;
essay: jokes optional, yet each minute has one small pleasure (a tally, a second look, a "see
that?"). Lists: N straight items and one absurd one, in the same grammar.

## Ending

No invented sponsor or brand beat. A specific integration the user asks for sits outside the clip-react cycles and ahead of the heartfelt close. Otherwise, once the heartfelt
paragraph is done, a short in-character sign-off (subscribing pitched as signing up to a running
joke the host invented, never pleading; or a gag about the next video), under 20 s, or skip it.

## Anti-patterns

- "Today we'll be looking at" previews: show the structure by doing it; a preview is allowed only
  when it is the joke.
- Neutral recaps: every description carries a stance or a joke.
- Sales voice or manufactured excitement, at any point.
- Leaning on cliffhangers: viewers stay for packed, rising material, not for things kept from them.
- Essay superlatives with nothing behind them: each "best" must be earned by a tally, a term of art or a second viewing.
- Explaining a joke (except a deliberately named callback).
- Borrowing any real creator's trademarks (their hello, what they call their fans, their end music,
  their stock phrases).

## Short-form mode

At 4 minutes or less, drop the long-form maps; keep the peer voice, self-mockery before a jab, and
the harm check.
- About 150 words a minute from the host: 3 minutes gives roughly 350-420 of the host's words, with
  clip time on top; clips shrink to 8 s or less.
- Sentence one names the artifact and the host's view; skip the sketch, the greeting and the
  origin story.
- Two or three clip-react cycles at most, least to most absurd, each cut to clip → one line that restates or questions → one acted bit or tag.
- The heartfelt point becomes a single final sentence, but it never disappears.
- One loop or callback at most; no scaffold announced, no turn in the middle.
- A timestamp every 15-30 s; a 3-5 line retention map; an outro of one line, or no outro.
- Vertical Shorts: the artifact is already playing inside 2 s; the stance line works without sound; a
  loop-close is worth trying.

## Output format

```
# <Working title> - <lane>, ~<N> min

## Hook options (pick one)
### Hook 1 - <type>
### Hook 2 - <type>
### Hook 3 - <type>
(each 40-80 words)

## Script
[0:00] spoken text; a timestamp every 60-90 s of runtime (15-30 s in short-form)
[CLIP: ...] [ACT-OUT: ...] [B-ROLL: ...] each cue on its own line

## Retention map
[m:ss] a line for each escalation marker, callback plant or loop
```

Titles and headings never report the script's own pacing or rule keeping (word counts, a note that
the view arrives early); the retention map names devices, not grades.
