## Hook types (nine)

Within 30 s each of these leaves the viewer with a concrete image and an open question that the
script has committed to resolve.

| | Type | Mechanics | Invented shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Object | A physical thing, one absurd figure, then tension. The most common opening in the genre. | "This is a concrete canoe. It floats. It should not." |
| B | Misconception reversal | Say what everyone believes, then turn it over; the viewer's prior knowledge becomes the villain. | "Those dates on milk cartons are not about safety." |
| C | What-if | The question at once, made concrete, inflated to absurdity, then walked back. | "What if everyone on Earth jumped at once? Surely an earthquake. Well, almost not." |
| D | Impossible thing | A record plus a broken intuition. | "The biggest animals should be full of tumours. They are not." |
| E | Mid-climax | Open inside the peak footage, then rewind: the rewind is the promise. | "This is me in a pool of packing foam. To see why, go back eight months." |
| F | Confession | The host admits a strange, self-inflicted cost or obsession, then gives the backstory. | "Six years on one spinning coin." |
| G | Casualty puzzle | A dated, located run of unexplained losses; explaining the cause takes the whole runtime. | "In one winter, a town kept losing its cattle." |
| H | Dare | Address the viewer's ego directly. | "Stop here if you like your instincts as they are." |
| I | Authority shock | An institution's prediction of disaster and the claim we are on track (only a real, sourced prediction). | A dated forecast checked against today. |

**Boosters:** a montage of news quotes 20-40 s in, to show the topic is real and disputed; and a
spoken list of questions right after the hook (five in one case, always three in another). Such a list is a pledge: every item on it is a loop the video has agreed to close.

## Skeleton and variants

| % | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0-5 | Hook: picture plus question |
| 5-12 | Stakes and contract: why it matters, the questions, what the viewer will understand by the end |
| 12-30 | Primer: the least mechanism needed, through one anchor analogy (simple machines before engines; small-scale trade before national data) |
| 30-70 | Ladder: the same mechanism at growing scale or across cases (sorted by size; up through kinds of infinity; layer by layer; following the money stop by stop) |
| 60-75 | Twist: the hook's promised reversal arrives; can include a staged false ending ("credits roll... not yet"), or a build-up to disaster that turns out to change almost nothing |
| 85-95 | Wider view: the subject reframed against the species, deep time or who the viewer is; nearly universal, and where videos get shared |
| 95-100 | Nothing, or a single pointer to another video |

**Ramp rule:** each rung uses the vocabulary the previous rung taught. A tangent is fine when the later steps truly need it, and the script tells the viewer so ("all of
this is about to matter"): leave a visible
promise and repay it. Tangents end with a snap-back line returning to the title's question.

**Inquiry-led:** evidence arrives in the order the investigator met it, with interviews as beats;
checkpoint questions reset attention; each answer to the opening questions produces the next scene.

**Host-led:** a quest in order (the dream → numbered failures → a montage → test day → the payoff), and the science section placed only after the viewer cares.

**Voiceover essay:** a thesis-driven chain of causes, one link a paragraph, with the twist around the
fifth link.

## Retention devices

- **Promises in the open:** "we'll get to that"; always kept, usually in the same act.
- **Stacked questions:** several fired before any is answered; a three-question roadmap turns the
  table of contents into a cliffhanger.
- **The "but" puncture every 60-120 s:** assert a resolution, then pierce it ("that's the theory,
  anyway"; "or is it?"). No stretch passes 90 s without one.
- **Stakes inside abstraction:**
    1. *Make it physical for the viewer:* a commute measured in cigarettes' worth of smoke; a question
     about why coffee never un-stirs, asked of strangers.
    2. *Cost or casualties to an absurd decimal:* exact figures read as spectacle; round ones read as
     filler.
  3. *Collapse time:* a future made personal by a comparison with today.
- **False summit:** script the whole wrong conclusion, then demolish it; or let a catastrophe
  inflate fully before it deflates. Viewers sense the remaining runtime and expect the reversal.
- **The bargain:** spectacle first, then an open request for a short stretch of technical detail.
- **Proof by demonstration:** stage the test (a cash prize offered to whoever manages to drive the doctored trolley; buying the objects rather than quoting the maker). Only tests that really happened.

## Analogy and making it concrete

- **Four moves:** signal the analogy → keep to the everyday object's rules for two to four
  jargon-free sentences → come back with the technical term attached → reuse it as shorthand from
  then on (once a train that adds cars at the front and drops them at the back exists, everything
  later is told in train terms).
- **Negotiated analogies:** let host and guest argue the comparison on camera and refine it, then
  summarise it again at the end; the argument is the explanation.
- **Personify the invisible:** emotional verbs for cells, economies, failed stars.
- **A household ruler for scale:** buses, canyons, marathons, wallets; never a large number alone.
- **Calibrate sensations:** name a familiar feeling, then correct it.
- **Admit the limit:** say where the analogy fails; the honesty buys trust for the next one.

## Payoff rhythm

| Style | Rhythm |
|---|---|
| Animated science narration | a quotable wonder each 30-50 s; nearly every paragraph ends on a line that lands; the final payoff is a mood, not a fact |
| Fast systems essay | an insight or joke almost every sentence (about 1,000 words for 4.5 minutes); alliteration and rhyme as micro-payoffs |
| Philosophical tangents | long loops of 2-3 minutes, nested so an inner payoff lands while the outer loop stays open; short verdict lines |
| Field investigation | alternates a mechanism revealed with a host reaction, roughly each 60-90 s |
| Build or engineering host | emotional payoffs (reactions, slow-motion reveals) each 30-60 s; the science in one guarded block of about 2 minutes |
| Money and supply-chain essays | an ironic reversal at paragraph ends, about each 40-70 s; a joke stands in when the ladder runs long |

Rule across the genre: every 90 s holds at least one reveal, twist or laugh. The biggest fact lands near
70 percent; the end carries the meaning.

## Quick card

- **Hook (first 30 s):** object, reversal, what-if, impossible thing, mid-climax or casualty
  puzzle; optionally a short news montage or a dare.
- **Contract (0:30-1:30):** why it matters to the viewer personally, three questions in order, the
  promise of the reveal.
- **Beats:** 12-30 primer through one analogy → 30-70 ladder, each rung reusing the last one's
  words, a "but" turn at least every 90 s → 60-75 turn, maybe a false ending first → 85-95 wider
  view → last 5, one line at most, in keeping with the rest.
- **Analogy:** signal → stay inside the object → attach the term → reuse → admit where it breaks.
- **Register:** "you" by default, "I" as a fellow learner; present tense, contractions; building
  clauses that land on a short verdict; show, then name, then defuse; a household ruler for every
  big number; exact over round; tests over assertions; a reveal, twist or laugh each 90 s;
  hedge mid-video only; close every loop; treat the wrong belief as reasonable.
