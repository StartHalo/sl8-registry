## Hook types (ten)

Whatever the type, the viewer knows the promise by 0:15-0:30.

| # | Type | How it works | Example shape (invented) |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Results blitz | Lead with an outsized, exact result and its time span, then offer the condensed lesson. | "Eleven years, six exits; the last one closed at 41.3 million." |
| H2 | Roadmap | Show the numbered map before any content, then begin level one. | "Four stages from zero to your first hire. Stage one: cut the noise." |
| H3 | Paradox | Two trends that should not both be true; the video exists to reconcile them. | "Nobody can afford a flat, yet every flat sells. Who is buying?" |
| H4 | Mirror | Describe the viewer's week in such detail they feel seen; second person, present. | "It is midnight, the phone is at your nose, your thumb keeps going." |
| H5 | Inversion | Teach the wrong outcome on purpose, as a mock tutorial, then flip it. | "Want to stay broke for ever? Easy. Step one: ..." |
| H6 | Story under way, with a price | Open in the middle of something the host just did, with its exact cost. | "I just drove off in a 39,000-dollar car. Here is what it did to my budget." |
| H7 | Gate | Name the exact painful state; the matching viewer is told to stay. | "If you wake up tired however long you sleep, stay." |
| H8 | Answer-first | Give the full answer in 15 seconds, then promise to go deeper. Confidence is the hook. | "Here is the whole routine in one breath. Now the why." |
| H9 | News collage | Thesis line, a fast run of news clips showing the topic is live, then the signature pivot. | Clips carry the urgency for the host. |
| H10 | Borrowed legend | Open on a well-known person's anecdote that already contains the thesis. | Name a public figure only with a sourced story; never invent one. |

**Specificity.** Vague money words ("loads", "a fortune") are absent from strong hooks. Results are exact and not rounded
(precision reads as receipts), and usually per unit of time or per item, which makes them feel
auditable. With no personal figures, a named third-party statistic stands in.

## Beat maps

**Host-led list (8-20 min):**

| Beat | % | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook and promise | 0-4 | H1, H2 or H7 plus the item count |
| Standing and ground rules | 4-10 | who the host is, terms used, an honest disclaimer that passive money is never fully passive |
| Item loop x N | 10-85 | per item: title, how it works, an example, a figure, a short ruling |
| Optional act break | about 20 | a single break, placed once an item is complete |
| Escalate or recap | 85-95 | the weightiest item closes the list, or all N are recapped |
| Next step | 95-100 | playlist, linked video or comment prompt |

Later items get roughly half the time of the first; acceleration makes momentum. A fixed scoring
rubric across items (for example: effort to start, time to the first 100 a month, effort to keep
it going) turns a list into a comparison and gives every segment the same predictable shape.

**Voiceover essay (about 10-25 min):** cold-open paradox or mirror (0-8) → signature pivot line
(8-12) → context and history (10-30) → mechanism or unit economics, the core "how it works"
(30-60) → complication or data turn (60-85) → thesis as a maxim (85-95) → pointer to related
content (95-100). Comparative variant: three companies in one industry at three price points, each walked through its customers, prices, per-unit money and a ruling on strategy; later cases
point back at earlier ones.

**Spoken direct-response talk:** the count up front → each point carried by a story with its own
name → restate the count halfway and again at the close → a live demonstration as the finale → a soft
close towards the next piece of content.

**Introducing a framework:** give it a proper name (so it can be owned), count its parts, preview
them all in one breath, walk the parts saying the count each time, and finish every part with a brief ruling. The
numbering works as the viewer's progress bar.

## Retention devices

- **List momentum:** say N early, repeat "number k" at each joint, and signal when the list is
  nearly done.
- **Open loops:** a preview that is deliberately puzzling now and pays off later; the loop can even
  name itself ("this spreadsheet story is going somewhere").
- **Last-one-matters-most:** the heaviest material is held for the end and flagged; a taxonomy can
  escalate verbally towards its worst member; the title's arithmetic is held to the final third.
- **Personal stakes at peak abstraction:** teach the mechanism, insert "this happened to me", then
  return to the mechanism with trust earned (only with a real story from the user).
- **Two proof orders:** host-led runs claim then proof inside the same minute; voiceover runs data
  then verdict. Either way, any claim gets its backing (a figure, a named source, the host's own proof) within roughly 30
  seconds.
- **Interaction beats:** rhetorical questions answered for the viewer; a mid-video challenge
  ("pause and guess the figure").
- **Pattern breaks:** a mock public-information narrator, a stunt cold open, a bonus after the
  outro.

## Proof systems

Channels usually combine two of these five:
1. **Results ledger:** exact income or net-worth figures with time spans, disclosed unprompted;
   the disclosure is itself content.
2. **Evidence label:** the host reads research and books on camera and ties each claim to them, explained at the
   level of mechanism rather than bare instruction.
3. **Named third-party data:** listing platforms, census figures, industry bodies, filings;
   arithmetic the viewer could redo is the authority.
4. **Confession:** an admission that cost the host something, placed so it cannot pass for a
   humble-brag, and always turned into a lesson. Viewers trust this beat most.
5. **Anti-hype disclaimer:** calling out the get-rich sellers in the niche builds trust.

Money figures: exact, usually not round, per week, month or unit, and given in before-and-after
pairs. Cent-level figures signal receipts. Large numbers are made human by comparison.

## Direct-response toolkit

- **Command bursts:** short orders to "you", in the present, without softeners.
- **Drill by repetition:** repeat the same short instruction across days; the repetition performs
  the discipline.
- **If-then:** what follows "then" is something to do or something that happens, not an emotion.
- **Cost of inaction:** a vivid, dated future ("five years pass either way").
- **Conditional guarantee:** the condition is the behaviour the host wants.
- **Script-giving:** hand the viewer the exact words for the conversation they fear.
- **Objection as dialogue:** voice the pushback, answer it at once.
- **Maxim minting:** each major beat earns one original slogan that viewers repeat and share.
- **Live value stack:** add real bonuses one at a time and ask for a commitment after each. Use
  only with an offer the user supplied and asked to feature (router monetization policy); never
  invent scarcity.

Lower-intensity hosts borrow the imperative register softly; some turn it into invitations.

## Quick card

- **Hook:** one of the seven guide types, 30 seconds or less, with an exact figure or a named
  contradiction.
- **List beats:** 0-4 hook and count; 4-10 one boast plus one confession, terms, disclaimer;
  10-85 item loop, shrinking; one act break at about 20 or about 85, never both; 85-95 heaviest
  last or recap; 95-100 free next step.
- **Essay beats:** paradox (0-8) → pivot (about 10) → history (to 30) → mechanism (to 60) → data
  turn (to 85) → maxim → pointer.
- **Retention:** N early and at each joint; a loop per act, paid off on screen; the 30-second
  backing rule; a real personal story where the ideas get most abstract; weightiest last; a
  pattern break each 5 minutes; answer the viewer's spoken objection.
- **Register:** first person to "you", present tense, contractions; explain long, judge short;
  confessions carry lessons; commands unhedged; one maxim a section; exact, non-round,
  per-unit figures in before-and-after pairs.
- **Close:** any like-ask is a joke or a favour to others; finish by pointing at the next thing
  to watch or do; a single plain subscribe or next-video line is the ceiling.
