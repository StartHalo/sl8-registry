## Intake

Collect only what the brief does not already give; anything still missing takes the default
named here, and the delivery says which defaults were used (no question tool, HR12).

1. **Format.** Pick one master structure:
   - **Host-led list or how-to** (host in frame, 8-20 min): a counted set of items, steps or
     frameworks.
   - **Voiceover business essay** (10-28 min): the story of a company, an industry or an economic
     pattern.
   - **Direct-response talk** (any length): dense, unhedged advice spoken straight to camera.
   Default: host-led list when the brief promises steps or items; essay when it names a company or
   market; a kit or brief may name another.
2. **Receipts.** Exact, checkable figures from the creator's own life (income, savings, time
   taken, losses). The genre leans on them. If the user has no figures, the proof layer is named outside
   data (studies, filings, reports), and the script says so; no first-person figure is invented.
3. **One confession.** A real, costly admission (a failure, a limit, an embarrassment) to sit
   beside the boast. It earns more trust than any other beat. If the user offers no confession, the beat is left
   out and the delivery lists it as missing; it is never made up.
4. **Length and the count.** Target minutes (about 140-150 spoken words a minute) and how many
   items, steps or lessons the title promises. Default length: the shortest typical length of the
   chosen format (8 min list, 10 min essay). At 4 minutes or less, use Short-form mode below.

## Hooks

Write three, each from a different row, each 30 seconds or less and 40-80 words. Every hook
carries an exact number or a named contradiction. Precise, non-round figures, stated per unit of
time or per item, read as evidence ("$46.2M", "$78 a month"); "six figures" or "a lot" do not.

| Type | Shape (our wording) |
|---|---|
| Receipts | The host states an exact result and the time it took, then offers the short version of how. |
| Roadmap | The host names the map to an outcome, counts its steps, and starts step one at once. |
| Paradox | Two true facts that seem to cancel out, then the open question of how both hold. |
| Mirror | The viewer's own week described back to them, second person, present tense. |
| Inversion | Mock instructions for the bad outcome (how to stay broke), played straight. |
| Gate | Name the exact painful situation; anyone in it is told this video is theirs. |
| Answer-first | The whole answer inside 15 seconds, then a promise to go underneath it. |

## Structure by format

**Host-led list (percent of runtime):**
- **0-4:** hook plus the counted promise.
- **4-10:** standing: one boast and one confession within a minute of each other, the terms the
  video uses, and a plain warning that no shortcut exists.
- **10-85:** the item loop. Per item: say its number, give it a name (a proper noun for a
  framework), explain how it works, tell a story or example, give one exact money or time figure,
  close with a one-line verdict. Each item runs shorter than the one before; the speed-up is the
  momentum. Optional: rate every item on the same two or three axes so the list becomes a
  comparison.
- **At most one act break,** after a finished item, at about 20 or about 85 percent, never both.
- **85-95:** save the weightiest, most personal item for last and warn that it is heavy, or recap all N.
- **95-100:** point the viewer at something free to do next (a next video, a small task, a comment
  prompt). Never close on thanks alone.

**Voiceover essay:** paradox open (0-8) → a recurring signature line that turns into the story
(about 10) → history and context (to 30) → how the money works, with figures the viewer could
check (to 60) → the turn where deeper data complicates it (to 85) → the thesis pressed into a short
maxim → a pointer to related content. For comparisons: a single industry with a cheap, a middle and a premium company, each taken through audience, pricing, unit economics and verdict.

**Direct-response talk:** state the count first → carry each point with a story that has its own
name → recap the count at the middle and again at the end → a live demonstration or a stacked
offer as the climax (only a real one the user supplied; never invent an offer) → a soft close that
says the upstream material is free.

## Register and retention

- First person addressing "you", present tense, contractions. Past tense only inside a story that
  serves a present lesson.
- Pair a longer sentence that explains with a short one that judges; the short one is the line a
  viewer would clip. Coin one original maxim per major section.
- Nothing claimed goes 30 seconds without backing: the creator's own figure, a source named aloud, or something shown working. Host-led formats claim then prove; voiceover formats prove then claim.
- Repeat "number k" word for word at every joint, and signal the approach to the end.
- One open loop per act, closed on screen. Holding back the title's figure until the last third,
  then breaking it down line by line, works well.
- Call back only to what this script set up. Never "as I promised" or "remember that story"
  without the earlier line in this script.
- At the most abstract point, drop in a first-person story, then return to the mechanism.
- Voice the viewer's objection in their words and answer it straight away.
- Commands are plain imperatives; hedging lives in one disclaimer beat, not inside the commands.
- Make the cost of doing nothing concrete and dated.
- One pattern break (a skit, a stunt, a mock narrator) about every 5 minutes.
- Where it helps, give the viewer the exact words to say.

## Ending

No sponsor, plug, course, affiliate line or other monetization unless the user asked for that
specific one; then it stays clear of the teaching itself. Otherwise close on a next step that costs
nothing. A like or subscribe line, if asked for, is one line, played as a bit or as a favour to
other viewers, never a plea.

## Anti-patterns

Remove each of these from the draft:
- More than one sentence of warm-up; the viewer hears the promise before 0:30.
- Round or vague figures; where exact is impossible, give a bracket of exact values.
- Statistics without a source; quotes nobody owns.
- Guru posture: success never sounds quick or easy; say what it really cost.
- Even pacing; segments must speed up.
- A tease with no on-screen payoff.
- Corporate register: runs of passive voice, "one must".

## Short-form mode

At 4 minutes or less, the long-form map above does not apply. Keep exact figures, receipts inside
30 seconds and no guru posture.
- Budget about 150 words a minute: 3 minutes is about 420-470 spoken words.
- A single lesson backed by a single receipt and a single command. The number is in the hook's
  first sentence; boast and confession become one line apiece, said one after the other.
- A count of 3 or fewer, or no count for a one-mechanism video.
- Map: number hook (0-10) → boast and confession (10-20) → how it works, plus its proof (20-75)
  → the command or if-then (75-90) → a one-line verdict or maxim, then stop.
- One loop only (holding back the title figure's breakdown still works). At most one plain outro
  line.
- A timestamp every 15-30 seconds; a retention map of 3-5 lines.
- Vertical Shorts: the number on screen within 2 seconds; the hook works with the sound off; a
  loop-close is worth trying.

## Output format

```
# <Working title> - <format>, ~<N> min

## Hook options (pick one)
### Hook 1 - <type>
### Hook 2 - <type>
### Hook 3 - <type>
(each 40-80 words with an exact figure)

## Script
[0:00] flowing spoken text; each verdict sentence set apart
[B-ROLL: ...] [ON-SCREEN: $3,912.40] [CHART: ...]

## Retention map
[m:ss] a line for each open loop, count joint or pattern break
```

Claims about the script go in the script. Titles and headings never praise its own pacing or rule
keeping; the retention map alone comments, and it names devices, never grades.
