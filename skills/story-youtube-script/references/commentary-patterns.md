## Hook types (twelve)

What every strong opening shares: by the half-minute mark the viewer can say what is being judged
and which way the host leans.

| # | Type | Mechanics | Invented shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Sketch open | Start mid-joke with no context; the subject surfaces from the bit, then the voice drops back to normal. | The host in costume as a herald announcing the topic. |
| H2 | Rotating show name | A fixed hello, then a made-up programme title for this episode that hints at its subject. | "Welcome back to Kitchen Gadget Tribunal." |
| H3 | Artifact first | The odd thing itself, raw, before any frame; then the reaction. | Reading machine-written ad copy aloud word for word. |
| H4 | How it found me | The material arrived through the audience, which makes the stakes shared. | "Three hundred of you sent me this." |
| H5 | Costly experiment | A big, irreversible commitment stated up front. | "I am spending a month's rent to find out if these shows are staged." |
| H6 | Ruling first | The conclusion, flatly, then the work of earning it; in essays the ruling can be praise. | "This reboot does not work. Here is why." |
| H7 | Deadpan definition | One sentence of dictionary voice about an everyday thing, then the first undercut within about 20 s. | "Small talk is, broadly, a handshake made of weather." |
| H8 | Fake service | Sincere-sounding help that turns into anti-help. | "Many of you find this hard, so I made a quiz." |
| H9 | One question | A single concrete question the video answers. | "Can one re-edit fix this film's ending?" |
| H10 | Superlative object | One artifact, one extreme claim (mock-superlatives in the satirical lane). | "Of every prop in the series, this one bothers me most." |
| H11 | Confession | An embarrassing true fact as the entry ticket (only one the user supplied). | "I arrived not knowing a word of the language." |
| H12 | Announcement loop | Housekeeping turned into a reason to stay ("there is news at the end"). | Used before the sketch. |

## Beat maps

**Comic commentary (15-30 min):**

| % | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | Sketch open, greeting ritual or show name (H1, H2) |
| 3-10 | How the artifact arrived, the host's standing, today's premise; an optional announcement loop |
| about 10 | The host says out loud that the real part starts |
| 10-80 | Cycles of clip and reaction, rising in absurdity |
| 40-60 | One turn: an essay-like aside on a deeper cause, or a real event that freezes the experiment and becomes an act break |
| 80-90 | Verdict and the one sincere paragraph |
| 90-100 | Subscribe lore, a joke tease of the next video, the outro runner |

**The cycle (30-90 s):** 5-20 s of source → plain restatement ("several things are going on at
once") → logic questions → an act-out or imagined scene in character voices → tag → next. Reaction
time matches or exceeds clip time; footage never plays past about 20 s unbroken.

**Deadpan mock-explainer (2-8 min):** opening definition (first tenth) → a ranking system the host
lays out in advance (grades, rungs, a mock org chart) → each item a set-up-and-twist unit of 15-25 s → a last flip that points the joke at the host or the audience (the highest grade of warning sign
turns out to be you). The list is the act
structure, rising tiers are the escalation, and the self-implicating end is the third act.

**Sincere essay (6-9 min):** the artifact cold (0-10) → an "I think" thesis by about 15 → context
(15-30) → evidence beats that get finer (30-75): count, name the technique, replay and close-read,
outside corroboration → widen to the largest human meaning in the last fifth → a direct-address
closer → a chatty personal sign-off.

**Interleaving:** source and reaction alternate in thin layers, and each analysis recasts what was
just shown. Nothing is watched twice for the same reason.

## Comedy mechanics

- **Spacing:** the deadpan lane reverses about every 15-25 s, the twist placed as the last words of
  a calm sentence ("last-word detonation"); set-ups are never flagged as jokes and the delivery
  stays flat. Comic commentary spaces big laughs 30-60 s apart and fills the gaps with act-outs and
  one-word tags.
- **Misdirection lists:** a run of genuine entries ending in a silly one built exactly like them,
  delivered in the same tone.
- **Fake precision:** deadpan pseudo-statistics sell irony; real figures can be played for
  absurdity. Only invented hypotheticals get fake precision; real claims need sources.
- **Callbacks at three levels:** in-video (plant the oddest line, return to it two or three times
  unannounced, name it at the climax); series runners (audience nickname lore, recurring jingles);
  between channels (a mock feud). Use only runners the user owns.
- **Absurd escalation:** read the clip at face value until it contradicts itself → inflate it
  into an acted hypothetical → push it to a world-scale consequence.
- **Self-mockery placement:** at the hook, at each judgement of someone else, and in the call to
  action, so criticism reads as teasing between peers. State the ethical line before documenting
  someone's genuinely harmful behaviour.
- **Understatement:** pretend kindly that an outrage was a slip ("surely a typo"), or concede the
  subject's point with obvious sarcasm.
- **Act-outs:** voice what people on screen are really saying, in their own mouths. Comic hosts lean on this shape more than any other.

## Essay mechanics

- **Thesis placement:** the artifact first, the thesis inside 60-90 s, framed as personal
  fascination; it is a lens, and the verdict waits for the end.
- **Evidence ladder:** (1) count something concrete before interpreting (words in an answer, shots
  in a sequence, seconds of a pause); counting buys trust; (2) attach a named technique from film or
  rhetoric with a quick definition, so viewers leave with a tool; (3) corroborate from outside (a
  study, an editor's account, the director's words); (4) only then interpret, still hedged.
- **The replay:** play the moment cold → analyse → play it again with the new frame. The climax is
  often a recasting, and how the viewer first saw the moment is itself the proof.
- **Theme braiding:** each point links to some other point; nothing set up is left hanging.
- **Endings:** widen from craft to human stakes and turn to "you".

## Retention devices

- **Escalation labels:** joins promise the ladder still climbs ("it gets worse"; "this one takes the
  prize"). The most transferable device in the comic lane.
- **Live stakes resets:** real setbacks in experiment formats become act breaks.
- **Open loops:** a teaser at the hook, a held reveal mid-video framed as an award.
- **Joke chapter turns:** transitions that joke about being transitions, keeping the peer voice.
- **Micro-loops in the cycle:** each clip is framed as a puzzle and the host solves it; confusion
  first, resolution second.
- **Deadpan alternative:** no chapters or loops; density plus a known rising scaffold, with the best
  twist reliably last.
- **Essay alternative:** a curiosity gap on one tiny concrete detail with the promise of a replay;
  going through a sequence one shot at a time works like the deadpan lane's rising tiers.

## Quick card

- **Hook by 0:30:** sketch, show name, artifact first, how it found me, experiment, ruling first,
  definition, question or superlative; the viewer knows the artifact and the stance.
- **20-minute commentary:** 0-3 bit → 3-10 origin and promise → spoken turn → 10-80 cycles (clip 20 s
  or less, restate, question, act out, tag; least to most absurd; escalation labels) → 40-60 one turn
  → 80-90 verdict and a sincere paragraph → 90-100 subscribe lore, teaser gag, outro.
- **5-minute deadpan:** definition → scaffold → 12-18 units of 15-25 s → reversal onto the viewer →
  stop.
- **8-minute essay:** artifact → "I think" by 15 percent → count → name → corroborate → replay → human
  stakes → "you" closer → warm sign-off.
- **Register:** peer, first person, present; big claims hedged through the self; self-mockery at the
  hook, before every jab and in the call to action; confusion before resolution; seams shown;
  persona to the last second.
- **Density:** deadpan reversal every 15-25 s; comic big laugh every 30-60 s; essay one delight a
  minute; lists N straight plus one absurd; escalate literal → scene → world.
