## Intake

Fill gaps with the defaults below and name them in the delivery (HR12: no question tool).

1. **The case.** Facts, timeline and checked sources. Given only a case name, research the victims,
   the perpetrator, the timeline, the investigation and the legal outcome (SKILL.md step 9). Never
   invent a case fact; tag anything unconfirmed `[VERIFY: ...]`. If the record is too thin to tell
   the case, say so and stop rather than fill gaps.
2. **Lane:**
   - **Narrated story** (a storyteller facing the lens, 20-45 min): a single unbroken chronology
     that keeps its mystery back.
   - **Footage analysis** (any length, voiceover on interrogation, bodycam or court footage): the
     culprit is known early; the collapse of their account drives it. Only when real footage exists
     and the customer supplies or links it.
   - **Conversational** (30-60 min, a host doing something else at the same time): from childhood to
     conviction, with the host's personality forward.
   Default: narrated story; footage analysis only with supplied footage; a kit or brief may name
   another.
3. **Reveal order.** Never build suspense around a question the title has settled. A title that
   names the culprit → the suspense is the catching. A title that conceals them → the suspense is
   identity and method. Footage that shows them → the suspense is how their account breaks down.
4. **Legal status.** Before a verdict, "allegedly" discipline runs through every line.
5. **Length.** About 145 spoken words a minute. Default: 20 minutes. At 4 minutes or less, use
   Short-form mode.

## Hooks

Three options, each 45 s or less and 40-80 words, from different rows. A hook may carry the
content warning.

| Type | Shape (our wording) |
|---|---|
| Ranked against the archive | Among all the cases the channel has told (only a real count the user gives), this one sits near the top for a named quality. |
| Warning with an address | Graphic material comes at the end, so viewer discretion is advised; the warning says where the payoff lives. |
| Found artifact | Investigators recovered a recording or picture, and it will be shown late in the video. |
| The last ordinary minute | An exact time and date, the person doing something routine, and the fact they did not return. |
| Unlike any other | What sets this case apart: an unusual body of evidence or behaviour. |
| Perfect surface | A family or an evening that seemed ideal, until a date when someone close was hiding something. |
| Scene, then rewind | Half a minute of the most frightening moment, cut at its height; the ritual; restart at the beginning. |
| Raw audio first | An emergency call or jail phone call plays unnarrated; the speaker is named only afterwards (only real, released audio). |

The warning keeps viewers better than any other device in this genre: it spells out what leaving
would cost them and at what point it arrives.

## Structure: one case

1. **0-2:** hook and warning.
2. **2-5:** a fixed ritual (an original greeting or theme, the same each episode) closing on a clear
   boundary that signals the story is starting.
3. **5-20:** ordinary life first. Victims are people before they are in danger: marriages, jobs,
   hopes. Lead with the victim if the culprit's identity is held back; lead with the culprit
   (childhood, warning signs) if the title names them.
4. **20-45:** the anomaly and the investigation, told only through the eyes of those who found
   things out (the neighbour who noticed the open gate), never an all-seeing camera. A lead or a
   dead end every 2-4 minutes. Give the police's mistaken theory honestly before it collapses.
5. **45-55:** one tidy break between acts, a quiet stretch of police work that lets tension go before it climbs
   again. After this, nothing interrupts the second half.
6. **55-80:** climbing stakes: close every segment on something that contradicts, half-reveals, or
   turns with "it would later emerge that ...". Release identity in steps: who, then how, then where.
7. **80-90:** the payoff: a second telling from the inside (based on confessions), or the capture.
   Slow time down here; moment-by-moment sensory detail belongs only in this zone, and time moves
   fast everywhere else.
8. **90-97:** outcomes and sentences, quick, cool and exact. One precise figure lands here
   (seconds, money, a date).
9. **97-100:** the closing ritual, unchanged from episode to episode. Skip any recap; the telling
   already summed it up.

**Footage-analysis variant:** the crime and the culprit are fully disclosed by 8 percent → the
footage in date order, arranged as encounters with institutions that grow harder (a statement, then questioning that moves from friendliness through exhaustion to direct challenge,
then the arrest) → explain each interviewing method moments before the viewer watches it succeed
→ trial extracts with one decisive cross-examination → a plain
verdict card, no outro.

## Register and retention

- Spoken, not written: contractions, chains of "and so ... and then", dialogue paraphrased in the
  narrator's voice. No "moreover" or "furthermore".
- **Tense as camera:** background in the past; move into the present the moment someone discovers
  something.
- Turn to the viewer in every act: brief them, quiz them, or give them a choice.
- Foreshadow with a time marker and an unnamed change ("then that spring, it all turned").
- Keep identities behind neutral labels ("a man in his thirties", "the caller") until the reveal.
- **Fix exact numbers early, then let the lie hit the number late** (the account says ten minutes;
  the camera log says 58 seconds).
- Promises with an address ("we will return to this"; "she asks the same question eight times in
  this interview"); every one is paid on screen.
- Call back only to what this script set up; never "like I said earlier" without the earlier line.
- **Horror never sits beside horror:** alternate it with procedure, analysis, or (in the
  conversational lane only) humour. Jokes live between beats, never inside violence.
- Payoff rhythm: narrated story, a slow build with something small resolving each 2-4 minutes
  and one big release at 80-90 percent; footage analysis, a step up roughly each 5-10 minutes (a
  contradiction caught, the account changed, something admitted, the arrest, the verdict);
  conversational, the host comments about each half-minute (20-40 s).

## Ending

No invented sponsor, brand or plug. A specific integration the user asks for is placed at the act break, ahead of the second half.
Otherwise the narrated story closes on a short fixed outro ritual (an original sign-off, never a
real channel's), only after the outcomes beat; footage analysis stops flat on the verdict card. A
single plain line (the next episode, or an invitation to suggest a case) may close the video; the
story itself is never broken into.

## Ethics (always on)

These hold in every mode and every length, and double as trust devices.
- Victims are people before they are in peril; never invent what they thought as they died.
  Rebuild events only from what was confessed, testified or recorded, and tell the viewer which.
- Fence every speculation: "allegedly" before a verdict, "it is believed", "according to <named
  source>". Say out loud how confident the account is.
- Name what you leave out and why ("I am not naming the children, because ..."); giving the reason
  builds trust.
- Make killers small and pathetic, never legendary. No glamour, no blaming the victim.
- Name institutional failures where the record documents them.
- Describe how harm was done briefly, then move to its consequences; never dwell on gore.
- A content warning is a contract: give it aloud, with where the payoff sits.

## Anti-patterns

- An unresolved ending: the outcome is always delivered inside the video; no cliffhanger bait for
  the next one.
- A mystery about what the title already told the viewer.
- Ads or calls to action in the climax; recap padding at the end.
- Vague authorities ("experts say"): name the source, the technique, the document.
- An all-seeing dramatisation; a lurch into comedy during an atrocity; written-English register.

## Short-form mode

At 4 minutes or less, drop the long-form map. **The ethics section does not shrink away.**
- About 150 words a minute, so roughly 420-470 spoken words fill 3 minutes.
- Open directly on the hook: the last ordinary minute, or the perfect surface, in one sentence. No ritual and no preamble; if a warning is needed it shrinks to a clause ("a warning: this turns
  dark").
- One case, one reveal: ordinary life in one or two sentences (personhood shrinks but stays), the
  anomaly by 20 percent, one "it would later emerge" turn, and the outcome as the closing sentence.
- A single loop: the hidden identity or detail, raised in the opening and answered at 80-90
  percent. No
  second telling, no chapter foreshadowing.
- A timestamp every 15-30 s; a 3-5 line retention map; at most one plain outro line.
- Vertical Shorts: the hook works muted; a loop-close (the last line runs into the first) is worth
  trying.

## Output format

```
# <Working title> - <lane>, ~<N> min

## Hook options (pick one)
### Hook 1 - <type>
### Hook 2 - <type>
### Hook 3 - <type>
(each 40-80 words)

## Script
[0:00] narration as spoken text; dialogue paraphrased in the narrator's voice
[FOOTAGE: ...] [PHOTO: ...] [MUSIC STING] [RECONSTRUCTION B-ROLL: ...]
[VERIFY: a fact the customer must check against sources]

## Retention map
[m:ss] a line for each addressed promise, hidden identity, foreshadowing line or number collision
```

Titles and headings never rate the script's own pacing or rule keeping; the retention map names
devices, not grades.
