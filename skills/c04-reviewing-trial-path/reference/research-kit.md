# The research kit: three parts, always

Each part is something the founder runs with real people in under 30 minutes of their own time.
Adapt the wording to the product and to the open questions of this review (name the change IDs each
part would settle); keep the shape. Never simulate the answers. Ported from the v12 build.

## Interview script (qualitative)
- **Who:** 5 customers who bought in the last year; if possible 1–2 who tried and did not buy.
- **How:** 10-minute calls, or the same questions by email.
- **Ask:** 1. What was happening when you started looking for something like this? 2. What almost
  stopped you from signing up? 3. What did you compare us with? 4. What would you tell a colleague
  this does for you? 5. Was anything on our website confusing or missing?
- **Send back:** your notes, one paragraph per person, in their words where you can.

## Five-user test (user testing)
- **Who:** 5 people like your buyers who do not know the product.
- **How:** watch them share their screen; say nothing while they work.
- **Tasks:** 1. "Find out what this costs for a business like yours." 2. "Start a trial", stopping
  before submitting. 3. "Tell me in one sentence what this product does." Add one task for the
  highest-scoring change you could not judge from text.
- **Send back:** where each person hesitated, what they said out loud, and whether they finished.

## Recording setup (mouse tracking)
- **How:** add a free session-recording tool to the site (most take one snippet); exclude every page
  where people type payment or personal data.
- **Then:** after two weeks, watch 10–20 recordings of visitors who reached the pricing or sign-up
  page; note where they stopped.
- **Send back:** what you saw, one line per recording.

Optional, when the review could not see the screens: **Screenshots** of the home, pricing and
sign-up pages on a phone and a desktop, and of the first three screens after signing up.

Every part ends: send it back by running "Review our trial path" again with the notes attached.
