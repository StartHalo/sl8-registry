# Voice and messages

## Messaging is what the brand says; voice is how it says it (Wheeler)

The brand speaks with one distinctive voice everywhere, while its tone flexes with the moment
(Mailchimp's content style guide: "the same voice all the time, but your tone changes").

## Voice attributes: "X, not Y"

- Choose 3–5 attributes. Fewer can't guide a writer; more can't be remembered.
- Pair each with the overdone version of itself, so a writer knows where the line is:
  "Warm, not gushing" · "Direct, not curt" · "Calm, not sleepy" · "Expert, not clinical".
- Avoid attributes every brand claims ("friendly, professional, modern"). If a competitor from S1
  would pick the same word, find a sharper one or make the "not" do the work.
- Each attribute gets one **on-voice** and one **off-voice** example, written as real copy for
  this app (a reminder, a button, a subtitle). Rules without examples are ignored.

## Messages: one umbrella, three supports

An umbrella message is often supported by three subordinate messages (Wheeler, after Covello's
message mapping): people take in about three points. The umbrella says the promise; each
supporting message proves one core value from the brief, with its proof point.

## Checks

- Read every example aloud as the audience from S2. Would they say it, or roll their eyes?
- Cover the app's name: could a competitor's copy pass as this? (Neumeier's hand test.)
- No health claim slips into the examples; sort any with the health-claims limits in S2.
