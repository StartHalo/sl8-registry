# Gray's Cookies — the "smart" creative brief

Not an app, but the clearest public worked example of a single-minded brief. The article prints a bad and a smart version of every line; this file keeps the smart version only. The bad version of the same brief is a useful contrast (see `bad-1.md` for an app example).

## Objective
"Drive trial of Gray's Cookies, using the positioning of "The good tasting healthy cookie.""
Desired response: "Get consumers to TRY Gray's, believing the great taste will win them."

## Proposition
"Try Gray's Cookies, the great-tasting cookie without any guilt."
(Brand idea behind it: "Gray's are the best tasting yet guilt-free pleasure, so you can stay in control of your health and mind.")

## Reasons to believe
The source gives two, not three, and says to use "a maximum of two support points":
1. In blind taste tests Gray's matched the market leaders on taste, with only 100 calories and 2g of fat.
2. In a 12-week study, people who ate Gray's once a night as dessert lost 5 pounds.

## Audience
"Proactive Preventers." Suburban working mothers aged 35–40 who will "do whatever it takes to stay healthy". They run, work out and eat well, and food is their stress relief. Insight: once they cheat on a diet they feel guilty and keep cheating.

## KPI
— (the brief names the response, trial, but gives no number or date)

## Channels
A lead medium and a short support list: a 30-second TV ad as the main creative, supported by event signage and in-store display, with the idea carried into digital and social and a microsite.

## Budget
—

## Deliverables
The main deliverable is one 30-second TV ad, with supporting signage, in-store display, digital and social pieces, and a microsite. The author also suggests asking to see every creative idea as a TV script, a billboard and a long-copy print ad.

## Out of scope
— (no out-of-scope list. The mandatories are kept few and do not steer the creative: the line already on pack, the Whole Foods logo on 25% of print under the listing agreement, and the legal disclaimer for the taste test and the study.)

## Why it is good
- It has one objective (trial). It does not try for trial and usage frequency at once.
- One sentence carries one benefit, great taste without guilt. The bad version of the same line packs in seven claims.
- The proof points close the one gap a sceptic would raise (does it really taste good, and does it really help?). They are not a random list of claims.
- The audience is one named segment with demographics, behaviour and an emotional insight, not "18–65 years old".
- Weakness to fix in our version: there is no numeric KPI, budget or end date. This example sets the proposition bar, not the KPI bar.
