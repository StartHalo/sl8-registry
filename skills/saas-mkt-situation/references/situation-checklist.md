# S1 Situation: the checklist

SOSTAC's situation analysis (PR Smith) answers "where are we now?" through the customer, the
competition, the company and the outside world, with the key findings up front. For a B2B SaaS,
two disciplines come first inside it: start from the **best-fit customers and their
alternatives** (April Dunford), and record the **sales motion and price** (Rob Walling; Emily
Kramer's MKT1), because they decide which channels can work.

## Customers and segments

- **Best-fit customers first:** the kind of organisation that gets the most value fastest, and the
  person who buys (role) and the person who uses it, if different. Evidence: customer logos and
  case studies on the site, reviews, the founder's notes.
- Then 3–5 segments by situation or need (for example "multi-site practices with rota changes
  every week"), each with how they buy, where they look, and size where a source or the founder
  gives it; otherwise `[TBD]`.
- Talking to customers is the best source (Walling: prospects, customers, people who didn't buy,
  people who cancelled). If the founder sent notes, use their words. If not, add an open decision
  suggesting five short customer conversations and the three questions to ask.

## Competitors and alternatives

- **Alternatives** (Dunford): what a buyer would use if this product didn't exist. Usually a
  spreadsheet, paper, a general-purpose tool, an assistant's time, or doing nothing. Name them
  plainly; they are often the real competition for a small product.
- **Competitors, at most 5**, each under `### <name>`: who they're for in their own words, price
  and model (from their pricing page), what their page leads with, proof they show, and the gap
  they leave. Cite the URL beside each claim.
- See [alternatives-and-reviews.md](alternatives-and-reviews.md) for finding them.

## The company

- Product in one line; who it's for; what's different, in the site's words.
- **Price and billing** (from the pricing page or the founder), and roughly what a customer is
  worth a year.
- **Sales motion:** demo-led ("Book a demo"), trial-led ("Start free trial"), or hybrid; what
  happens after the button.
- **Channels in use** and what the founder says works.
- **Integrations and partners** shown on the site.
- **Proof:** customer count, logos, testimonials, review-site ratings (only ones you opened or
  the founder gave).
- **Funnel:** visitors, trials or demo requests, activated or demos held, paid, churn, with the
  period. Each one the founder's figure or `[TBD]`. "You can't outrun churn" (Walling): a churn
  figure matters more than a traffic figure.

## External factors

- **Buyer's calendar:** when budgets are set and decisions made. For buyers such as hospitals,
  local councils, universities or other public bodies, the budget year and approval season shape every
  timing choice in S5.
- Who signs, and anything buyers require before they buy (security, privacy, procurement
  thresholds), with a source or "(from you)".
- Market trends only with a source.

## Earlier strategy (only when one was given)

- What it decided: targets, positioning or message, channels, goals and budget, in its own words.
- For each: **holds** (evidence still supports it), **questioned** (evidence now points another
  way, and which evidence), or **untested**.
- This feeds the "what changed from your earlier strategy" section of the plan.

## Key findings (first in the file)

At most 5, each one sentence that would change what the plan does, most important first.
The last one names the likely growth obstacle, as a hypothesis for S3.

## Open decisions

Every `[TBD]` gets a line here: what is missing, and what the founder could send to fill it.
Start a line with `Blocking:` only if the plan can't go on without it. Write "- none" if there are
none.
