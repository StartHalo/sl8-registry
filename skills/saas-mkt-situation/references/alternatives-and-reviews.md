# Finding competitors and alternatives; reading reviews

## Finding them

1. The founder's list in the request or `inputs/`, first.
2. Web searches a buyer would run: "<category> software", "<problem> app for <buyer type>",
   "<product> alternatives", "<product> vs". "Alternatives" and "vs" searches show who buyers
   compare at the moment of choice.
3. Software review sites' category pages, when they open (see below).
4. Integration marketplaces of tools the product connects to: competitors are often listed there.

Pick the competitors buyers most likely compare against, not the biggest names in the category.

## Opening pages

- Open every page you cite with the web fetch tool, in this job. Cite the exact URL beside the
  claim, and list it under `## Sources` with today's date.
- A search-result snippet is not an opened page. Don't cite it; open the page or drop the claim.
- A page that won't load: say so in `## Open decisions` ("Couldn't open <url>; paste it to
  include it"). Never fill the gap from memory.

## Review sites

- Some review sites block automated reading. Use only pages that load normally in a fetch. Never
  try to get round a block, and never scrape many pages.
- When a review page won't load, use review text the founder pasted, or ask for "five recent
  reviews of you and of <competitor>" as an open decision.
- From reviews, take: the words buyers use for the problem, what they praise, what they complain
  about, and why they switched. Quote briefly, with the source.
